## Findings

### 1. Self-Scouting Allowed — Information Leak and Turn Drain
**Severity:** medium
**Type:** business logic

**Description:** The `scoutOpponent` function (pvpService.ts:106) does not check whether `attackerId === targetId`. The `challenge` function correctly has this guard at line 276, but scouting omits it. A player can scout themselves, spending 100 turns to see their own power rating, template breakdown, talent investment, and other derived stats.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Player sends `POST /api/v1/pvp/scout` with `{ targetId: "<own playerId>" }`.
2. Zod validates `targetId` as a UUID — passes.
3. `scoutOpponent` runs: spends 100 turns, queries the player's own equipment/skills/template, creates a `PvpScoutLog` entry where `scouterId === targetId`.
4. Player receives their own combat stats. A self-scout notification also appears in their own notifications.
5. Not a major exploit, but wastes turns by design flaw and pollutes scout notification history. More importantly, it establishes that any UUID is scourable — including UUIDs from players outside the rating bracket.

**Suggested Fix:** Add `if (attackerId === targetId) throw new AppError(400, 'Cannot scout yourself', 'SELF_SCOUT');` at the start of `scoutOpponent`.

### 2. Scout Bypasses Rating Bracket — Unlimited Opponent Intelligence
**Severity:** high
**Type:** business logic

**Description:** The `scoutOpponent` function does not validate that the target is within the attacker's rating bracket. The `getLadder` function filters by bracket (line 62-66), and `challenge` re-checks bracket (lines 338-342), but scouting has no such constraint. A player can scout ANY player in the game regardless of level or rating, as long as they know (or enumerate) their UUID.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. A low-rated player (1000 ELO) wants intelligence on a top player (2000 ELO).
2. The top player never appears on their ladder because they're outside bracket range.
3. Player obtains the top player's UUID (e.g., from leaderboard data, guild member lists, or PvP match history that includes UUIDs of attackers/defenders).
4. Player sends `POST /api/v1/pvp/scout` with `{ targetId: "<top player UUID>" }`.
5. Receives the top player's: combat level, attack style, armor class, power rating, template composition (offensive/defensive/supportive counts), max stamina, max mana, and talent tree investment depths.
6. Cost: only 100 turns per scout, no cooldown.

**Suggested Fix:** Add bracket validation to `scoutOpponent`, mirroring the bracket check in `challenge`. Also consider adding a scout cooldown per target (e.g., reuse PvpCooldown or a separate mechanism).

### 3. No Scout Cooldown — Repeated Information Harvesting
**Severity:** medium
**Type:** business logic

**Description:** There is no cooldown on scouting the same target. A player can scout the same opponent repeatedly, spending 100 turns each time. While the turn cost provides some protection, there's no mechanism to prevent spamming scouts against many targets to build a comprehensive intelligence database. Each scout also creates a `PvpScoutLog` notification for the target, which could be used to grief/spam a player.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Player runs a script sending `POST /api/v1/pvp/scout` in a loop with different target UUIDs.
2. Each call costs 100 turns (cheap relative to 64,800 bank cap).
3. Player maps out the template composition, power ratings, and talent builds of dozens of opponents.
4. Alternatively, repeatedly scouts the same target to spam their notification inbox with scout alerts.

**Suggested Fix:** Add a per-target scout cooldown (e.g., 1 hour). Also consider capping max unread scout notifications or deduplicating repeated scouts from the same player.

### 4. Durability Degradation and Resource Updates Outside Main Transaction
**Severity:** medium
**Type:** race condition

**Description:** The `challenge` function runs a Prisma `$transaction` (lines 389-469) that covers turn spending, bracket re-check, rating updates, match creation, and cooldown upsert. However, the following operations happen AFTER the transaction commits:
- Durability degradation for both attacker and defender (lines 473-476)
- Attacker HP/stamina/mana update (lines 482-507)
- Knockout/recovery state (line 495)
- Gold loss from flee (line 488-493)

If the server crashes between the transaction commit and these post-transaction updates, the player's resources won't reflect the combat outcome. The match record exists but the attacker's HP won't be reduced, and equipment durability won't decrease.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Player challenges an opponent and loses badly (HP goes to 0, should be knocked out).
2. Server crashes or request times out after the main transaction commits but before `setAllResources` / `enterRecoveringState` runs.
3. Match is recorded, ELO changes applied, turns spent — but attacker retains pre-combat HP.
4. Not directly player-exploitable (requires server crash), but creates inconsistent game state.
5. The defender's durability degradation (line 475) is also outside the transaction — meaning combat always damages the defender's gear even though the defender didn't initiate the fight.

**Suggested Fix:** Move resource/HP/durability updates into the main transaction, or implement a post-combat reconciliation check that runs on player's next action.

### 5. Defender's Equipment Degrades Without Defender Action
**Severity:** medium
**Type:** business logic

**Description:** When an attacker challenges a defender, the defender's equipment durability is degraded (line 475) based on the combat log. The defender has no say in this — they're offline, and their "ghost" (max resources) fights automatically. The defender's real equipment takes durability damage from a fight they didn't choose.

Combined with the fact that cooldown is only 6 hours and applies per-attacker (not globally), a defender could have their gear degraded by multiple different attackers in rapid succession.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Multiple alt accounts or coordinated players all challenge the same target.
2. Each gets a separate cooldown slot (cooldown is per attacker-defender pair).
3. Target's equipment durability degrades from each fight.
4. Target's gear can be broken without them ever playing, increasing their repair costs.

**Suggested Fix:** Consider one of: (a) remove defender durability loss from PvP, (b) cap total PvP durability loss per defender per day, or (c) add a global incoming-challenge cooldown per defender.

### 6. Combat Log Stored in Match Record Exposes Both Players' Full Stats
**Severity:** medium
**Type:** data leakage

**Description:** The `combatLog` field in the PvpMatch record (line 448) stores the full `TemplateCombatResult` including both combatants' maxHp, maxStamina, maxMana, remaining resources, potions consumed, damage-by-scaling-stat breakdown, and the per-round action log with exact damage values, hit/miss, crit rolls, action IDs, stamina/mana costs.

The `getMatchDetail` endpoint (line 588-621) returns this full `combatLog` to either participant. The `challenge` response (line 526) also returns the full combat result to the attacker.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Player A challenges Player B.
2. Player A receives the full combat result including:
   - Player B's exact maxHp, maxStamina, maxMana
   - Player B's exact damage values (reveals attack stat)
   - Player B's action sequence (reveals their combat template exactly)
   - Whether hits landed or were evaded (reveals defense/evasion stats)
   - Stamina/mana costs per action
3. This is strictly more information than scouting provides — scouting gives aggregate categories, but the combat log reveals the exact template action-by-action.
4. Player B can also view the log via `getMatchDetail`, getting identical information about Player A.

**Suggested Fix:** This may be partially by design (combat replays are a common feature). However, consider: (a) not exposing the opponent's remaining resources in the log, (b) anonymizing action IDs to categories, or (c) limiting the detail level for the losing side. At minimum, document this as intended behavior if it is.

### 7. ELO Calculation Can Produce Negative Ratings at Boundary
**Severity:** low
**Type:** business logic

**Description:** The `calculateEloChange` function (eloService.ts:9) computes `deltaB` as `Math.max(-ratingB, -deltaA) || 0`. The `|| 0` fallback handles the edge case where both operands are 0, but it also silently converts `-0` to `0` (JavaScript treats `-0 === 0` as true, and `0 || 0` is `0`). This is benign.

However, in pvpService.ts (line 405), `newAttackerRating` uses `Math.max(0, attackerRating.rating + attackerRatingChange)` to floor at 0, but this uses the PRE-TRANSACTION rating value (`attackerRating.rating`), not the fresh one read inside the transaction (line 394). If another concurrent challenge completed between the outer read and the transaction, the attacker's actual rating could differ, and the calculated `newAttackerRating` could be based on stale data.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Player A (rating 100) sends two challenge requests near-simultaneously.
2. Both read `attackerRating.rating = 100` outside the transaction.
3. First challenge wins: ELO +30, new rating = 130.
4. Second challenge loses: ELO -25, calculates `newAttackerRating = Math.max(0, 100 + (-25)) = 75`.
5. Second transaction OVERWRITES the first's rating 130 with 75.
6. Player effectively loses 55 rating from two fights instead of gaining 5.

The bracket re-check inside the transaction (lines 393-401) does re-read ratings, but the ELO delta and new rating are NOT recalculated from the fresh values — they use the stale outer values. The inner check only validates bracket, not rating staleness.

**Suggested Fix:** Recalculate `attackerRatingChange` and `defenderRatingChange` inside the transaction using `freshAttackerRating.rating` and `freshDefenderRating.rating`. Or use Prisma's `{ increment: attackerRatingChange }` pattern (which is already partially used for wins/losses) instead of computing absolute values.

### 8. Revenge Detection Does Not Verify Bracket
**Severity:** low
**Type:** business logic

**Description:** The revenge check (lines 345-354) looks for any recent match where the target attacked the current player, within a 7-day window. If found, the turn cost is halved (250 instead of 500). However, there's no validation that the revenge target is still within the player's rating bracket at the time of the original attack — only that they're within bracket NOW (line 340).

This means a player who was attacked by someone who has since moved far outside their bracket can still challenge them at the revenge discount, as long as the target happens to be within bracket. This is minor because the bracket check still applies.

**Exploit Scenario:** Not easily exploitable since the bracket check at line 340 still gates the challenge. The revenge flag only affects turn cost. Low risk.

**Suggested Fix:** No immediate fix needed. Document as intended behavior.

---

### 9. Notification Mark-Read Has No Pagination Limit
**Severity:** low
**Type:** input manipulation

**Description:** The `markReadSchema` accepts `matchIds: z.array(z.string().uuid()).optional()` with no `.max()` bound. A player could send an array of millions of UUIDs, causing a large `WHERE id IN (...)` query. Similarly, `scoutReadSchema` has `ids: z.array(z.string().uuid()).optional()` with no limit.

**Exploit Scenario:**
1. Player sends `POST /api/v1/pvp/notifications/read` with `{ matchIds: [<100,000 UUIDs>] }`.
2. Prisma generates `UPDATE pvp_match SET defender_read = true WHERE id IN (<100,000 values>) AND defender_id = '...'`.
3. Large IN clause may cause slow query or high memory usage.

**Suggested Fix:** Add `.max(100)` or similar to both `matchIds` and `ids` arrays in the Zod schemas.
