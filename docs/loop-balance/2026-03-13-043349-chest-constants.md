## System Overview

Chests are the reward for clearing an encounter site. The chest system has three layers:
1. **Chest rarity mapping**: encounter site size -> chest rarity (small=common, medium=uncommon, large=rare)
2. **Material rolls**: weighted random picks from the ChestDropTable, repeated N times
3. **Recipe chance**: independent roll for an advanced recipe unlock

Material rolls use `pickWeighted` on ChestDropTable entries. Each roll selects one item from the drop table using `dropChance` as the weight, then rolls quantity within `[minQuantity, maxQuantity]`. This is NOT an independent per-item chance -- it's a weighted pick from the pool, so exactly one item type is selected per roll.

## Analysis

### 1. Expected Material Rolls by Size

Material rolls follow a discrete uniform distribution `U(min, max)`.

| Size | Min Rolls | Max Rolls | E[Rolls] | Variance |
|------|-----------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Small | 1 | 2 | 1.5 | 0.25 |
| Medium | 2 | 4 | 3.0 | 0.667 |
| Large | 3 | 6 | 4.5 | 1.25 |

The ratio of expected rolls is small:medium:large = 1 : 2 : 3. This is a linear scaling, which pairs with the exponentially increasing combat cost (mobs scale roughly 3:6:12.25 expected). See the turn cost comparison below.

### 2. Material Rolls per Turn Invested

Using expected mob counts from ROOM_CONSTANTS analysis and ENCOUNTER_TURN_COST = 50:

| Size | E[Mobs] | E[Turn Cost] | E[Rolls] | Rolls/100 Turns |
|------|---------|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| Small | 3 | 150 | 1.5 | 1.00 |
| Medium | 6 | 300 | 3.0 | 1.00 |
| Large | 12.25 | 612 | 4.5 | 0.74 |

Small and medium sites have identical material-roll-per-turn efficiency (1.0 rolls per 100 turns). Large sites are 26% less efficient. This means large sites are strictly worse for material farming unless the player values the higher recipe chance or the specific items in the rare drop table.

### 4. Recipe Chance Analysis

| Size | Base Chance | Per-Site | Per-100-Turns | Sites to 50% Prob | Sites to 90% Prob |
|------|------------|---------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Small | 0% | 0% | -- | Never | Never |
| Medium | 2% | 2% | 0.67% | 35 | 114 |
| Large | 5% | 5% | 0.82% | 14 | 45 |

Using the geometric distribution, P(at least one recipe in N sites) = 1 - (1-p)^N:
- Medium: 50% probability after 35 sites, 90% after 114 sites
- Large: 50% probability after 14 sites, 90% after 45 sites

**Small sites can NEVER drop recipes.** This is a significant design choice. Players who only engage with small encounter sites (which is likely in tier 1 zones where Forest Edge families cap at medium) will never unlock advanced recipes through chest drops. They must either find medium/large sites or learn recipes through other means.

### 5. Full-Clear Interaction

Full-clear applies three bonuses to chests:
1. Size upgrade: small->medium, medium->large, large->large (capped)
2. DROP_MULTIPLIER (1.5x on roll count, ceil'd)
3. RECIPE_MULTIPLIER (1.5x on recipe chance)

**Effective full-clear chest values:**

| Original Size | FC Rarity | FC E[Rolls] | FC Recipe Chance | vs Base Rolls | vs Base Recipe |
|--------------|-----------|------------|-----------------|---------------|----------------|
| Small | uncommon | ceil(avg(2,4)*1.5) = 5 | 2%*1.5 = 3% | 3.33x | 0->3% |
| Medium | rare | ceil(avg(3,6)*1.5) = 7 | 5%*1.5 = 7.5% | 2.33x | 3.75x |
| Large | rare | ceil(avg(3,6)*1.5) = 7 | 5%*1.5 = 7.5% | 1.56x | 1.5x |

Note on the full-clear roll calculation: the code does `Math.ceil(baseRolls * FULL_CLEAR_CONSTANTS.DROP_MULTIPLIER)` where `baseRolls` is the already-rolled integer from `rollChestMaterialRolls`. So the actual rolls depend on the specific base roll:

| Base Roll | After 1.5x + Ceil |
|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 8 |
| 6 | 9 |

The ceil operation creates non-uniform amplification: odd base rolls get a larger percentage boost (1->2 is 2.0x, 3->5 is 1.67x) than even rolls (2->3 is 1.5x, 4->6 is 1.5x). This is a minor quirk but means full-clear reward variance is slightly asymmetric.

### 6. Encounter Size Distribution in Practice

From seed data, `pickEncounterSize` selects uniformly among allowed sizes between minSize and maxSize.

**Tier 1 (Forest Edge):** All three families have minSize=small, maxSize=medium. Distribution: 50% small, 50% medium, 0% large. No large sites exist in tier 1 -- players cannot access rare chests or recipe chances above 2% in the starting zone without full-clear.

**Tier 2+ zones:** Most families allow small-large (uniform 33/33/33). Some families are restricted:
- Treants in Deep Forest: small-medium only
- Golems in Crystal Caverns: medium-large only
- Undead/Abominations in Sunken Ruins: medium-large only

The weighted-by-discoveryWeight probability of encountering each size varies by zone. For Deep Forest:
- Wolves (100 weight, small-large): 33% small, 33% medium, 33% large
- Bandits (80 weight, small-large): 33% small, 33% medium, 33% large
- Treants (70 weight, small-medium): 50% small, 50% medium, 0% large

Blended size distribution for Deep Forest (weighted): 36% small, 38% medium, 26% large.

### 7. Material Drop Diversity per Roll

The weighted pick mechanism means each roll selects exactly ONE item type. With 4-6 entries in a typical drop table, higher roll counts don't just yield more items -- they also yield more diverse loot. The probability of seeing at least one of each item type increases with rolls.

For a drop table with K entries of equal weight, the expected number of distinct item types in N rolls follows the coupon collector variant:
- E[distinct items in N rolls] = K * (1 - ((K-1)/K)^N)

For a 4-entry common table with 1.5 rolls (avg): E[distinct] = 4 * (1 - 0.75^1.5) = 4 * 0.351 = 1.40 distinct items.
For a 6-entry rare table with 4.5 rolls (avg): E[distinct] = 6 * (1 - (5/6)^4.5) = 6 * 0.547 = 3.28 distinct items.

Caveat: drop tables don't have equal weights, so actual diversity depends on the weight distribution. Heavily skewed tables (one dominant item) reduce diversity.

### 8. Overflow Risk

The `rollAndGrantDropsTx` function respects inventory capacity. If `availableSlots` is provided and the player's inventory is near-full, drops go to overflow (pending loot). Each unique stackable template consumes one slot; non-stackable items consume one slot each.

With material rolls generating 1-6 distinct item templates, a full chest from a large site could consume up to 6 inventory slots. Players with fewer than 6 free slots risk losing some chest contents to overflow when opening a large chest. This interacts with INVENTORY_CONSTANTS but is worth noting as a constraint on the effective chest value.

## Issues Found

### Issue 1: Small Chests Have Zero Recipe Chance (Medium Severity)
CHEST_RECIPE_CHANCE_SMALL = 0 means small encounter sites can never yield recipe unlocks. In tier 1 (Forest Edge), families cap at maxSize=medium, so a player discovering a small site has BOTH fewer materials and zero recipe chance. Combined with the 50/50 size distribution in tier 1, half of all encounter sites in the starting zone are dead-ends for recipe progression. This creates a frustrating early-game experience where the reward for clearing a small encounter site is underwhelming.

### Issue 2: Large Sites Are Less Material-Roll-Efficient Than Medium (Low Severity)
Material rolls per turn invested: small=1.00, medium=1.00, large=0.74 per 100 turns. The roll count scales linearly (1:2:3) but mob count scales super-linearly (~3:6:12.25). The higher rarity drop table compensates when measuring total items, but the raw roll count doesn't keep pace with the turn cost increase. A player farming purely for materials should prefer medium sites over large sites, especially room-by-room where no rarity upgrade occurs.

### Issue 3: Full-Clear Ceil Asymmetry (Negligible Severity)
`Math.ceil(baseRolls * 1.5)` produces 2.0x for odd base rolls and 1.5x for even base rolls. The intent is a uniform 1.5x boost, but the ceil operation on the already-rolled integer creates a variable 1.5x-2.0x effective multiplier depending on the random base roll. This slightly favors low base rolls (1->2 is the largest relative boost).

### Issue 4: Drop Table Weight Imbalance Across Tiers (Low Severity)
Tier 1 common chests have 4 drop entries with total weight ~2.10. Tier 5 rare chests have 7 entries with total weight ~4.60. Higher tier rare tables dilute specialty mob drops with more item types, so the probability of getting any specific desired drop per roll decreases. A tier 5 rare roll has only a 13-16% chance of selecting a given specific item, vs 14-38% in tier 1 common tables. This makes targeted farming harder at higher tiers despite the increased roll count.

### Issue 5: No Chest Tier Above Rare (Design Limitation)
`getUpgradedChestSize('large')` returns `'large'` unchanged. There is no epic or legendary chest tier. This caps the reward ceiling for the hardest content. Full-clearing a large site in a tier 5 zone yields the same chest rarity as a large site in tier 2 -- the only difference is the drop table contents. This was already flagged in the ROOM/FULL_CLEAR analysis.

## Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Add Small Recipe Chance
Set CHEST_RECIPE_CHANCE_SMALL to 0.005 (0.5%). This gives small chests a tiny but non-zero recipe chance, ensuring players in tier 1 zones have some recipe progression path. At 0.5%, a player needs ~139 small sites for a 50% recipe probability -- still much slower than medium/large but not impossible.

### Recommendation 2: Scale Material Rolls to Match Turn Cost Curve
Increase CHEST_MATERIAL_ROLLS_LARGE to { min: 4, max: 8 } (E[6.0] vs current E[4.5]). This brings large site roll efficiency to 0.98 per 100 turns, closer to small/medium parity. The combined effect with rare table quantities still makes large sites the most rewarding in total items, while the raw roll count no longer feels punishing relative to the mob count.

### Recommendation 3: Use Floor-Half-Up Rounding for Full-Clear Multiplier
Replace `Math.ceil(baseRolls * 1.5)` with `Math.round(baseRolls * 1.5)` to produce a more uniform 1.5x multiplier. This changes: 1->2 (same), 2->3 (same), 3->5 (becomes 4 via rounding -- slight nerf), 4->6 (same), 5->8 (becomes 8 -- same), 6->9 (same). The only change is roll=3 produces 4 instead of 5, which is a minor nerf to medium full-clear chests.

Alternatively, apply the multiplier BEFORE rolling: multiply the min/max range by 1.5, then roll within the scaled range. This eliminates the ceil asymmetry entirely.

### Recommendation 4: Consider Tier-Scaled Chest Bonuses
Add a small per-zone-tier bonus to material rolls or recipe chance so that higher-tier zones offer better chest rewards beyond just the drop table contents. For example, +1 material roll per zone tier above 1. This would make tier 5 rare chests clearly superior to tier 2 rare chests in quantity, not just in which items appear.

## Risk Level

**low** -- The chest system is functional and internally consistent. The zero recipe chance on small chests is the most impactful issue but is arguably intentional design (forcing engagement with larger sites). The roll-efficiency disparity for large sites is masked by the higher per-roll item quantities from rare tables. No exploits or game-breaking issues exist in the chest constants themselves.
