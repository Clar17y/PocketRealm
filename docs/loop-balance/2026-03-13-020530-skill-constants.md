## Analysis

### 1. XP Curve: `xp = floor(100 * level^1.8)`

The 1.8 exponent produces a sub-quadratic power curve. Key milestones:

| Level | Total XP | Marginal XP (to next level) | Marginal growth rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 348 | 374 | - |
| 5 | 1,811 | 704 | - |
| 10 | 6,309 | 1,181 | - |
| 20 | 21,971 | 2,017 | ~1.7x vs lvl 10 |
| 30 | 45,584 | 2,772 | ~1.4x vs lvl 20 |
| 50 | 114,326 | 4,148 | ~1.5x vs lvl 30 |
| 70 | 209,496 | 5,418 | ~1.3x vs lvl 50 |
| 90 | 329,333 | 6,616 | ~1.2x vs lvl 70 |
| 100 | 398,107 | 7,138 | ~1.1x vs lvl 90 |

The marginal XP per level grows slowly. The ratio of `marginal(100) / marginal(2)` is only 7138/374 = 19x. Compare to a true quadratic (exponent 2.0) where the ratio would be 100/2 = 50x, or an exponential (e.g., `base * 1.1^level`) which would be orders of magnitude steeper. The 1.8 exponent creates a gentle curve that flattens at high levels relative to steeper alternatives.

**Scaling classification:** Sub-quadratic polynomial. The curve starts steep (early levels fly by) and gradually decelerates in growth rate. Derivative of `100 * L^1.8` is `180 * L^0.8`, which grows as L^0.8 -- slower than linear.

### 2. Efficiency Curve: `eff = 1 - (windowXpGained / cap)^2`

This quadratic decay creates smooth diminishing returns:

| Window % filled | Efficiency | Effective XP per 100 raw |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 100.0% | 100 |
| 10% | 99.0% | 99 |
| 20% | 96.0% | 96 |
| 30% | 91.0% | 91 |
| 40% | 84.0% | 84 |
| 50% | 75.0% | 75 |
| 60% | 64.0% | 64 |
| 70% | 51.0% | 51 |
| 80% | 36.0% | 36 |
| 90% | 19.0% | 19 |
| 100% | 0.0% | 0 |

**Integral analysis:** Total effective XP if you continuously earn raw XP up to the cap:

```
Integral of (1 - (x/cap)^2) dx from 0 to cap = cap * (1 - 1/3) = 2/3 * cap
```

This means the effective output per window is exactly **2/3 of the raw cap**, regardless of action size:

| Skill type | Window cap | Effective XP per window | Effective XP per day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combat | 7,000 | 4,667 | 9,334 |
| Gathering | 15,000 | 10,000 | 20,000 |
| Processing | 15,000 | 10,000 | 20,000 |
| Crafting | 15,000 | 10,000 | 20,000 |

### 3. Leveling Time Estimates

Assuming a player maxes out every window (continuous active play):

| Skill Level | Days (combat) | Days (non-combat) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.7 | 0.3 |
| 20 | 2.4 | 1.1 |
| 30 | 4.9 | 2.3 |
| 50 | 12.2 | 5.7 |
| 70 | 22.4 | 10.5 |
| 100 | 42.7 | 19.9 |

**Single level-up time at high levels:**

| Level | Marginal XP | Days (combat) | Days (non-combat) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 4,148 | 0.44 | 0.21 |
| 70 | 5,418 | 0.58 | 0.27 |
| 90 | 6,616 | 0.71 | 0.33 |
| 99 | 7,138 | 0.76 | 0.36 |

Even at level 99, a combat skill level-up takes less than 1 day of max grinding. This is notably fast for an endgame MMO.

### 4. Character Level Progression

Character XP = `floor(skillXpAfterEfficiency * 0.3)`. Uses the same `xpForLevel()` curve.

| Source | Daily char XP | Days to char level 50 | Days to char level 100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 combat skill | 2,800 | 40.8 | 142.2 |
| 3 combat skills | 8,400 | 13.6 | 47.4 |
| All 15 skills maxed | 80,400 | 1.4 | 5.0 |

A player who maximizes all 15 skills hits character level 100 in about **5 days**. Even training just 3 combat skills gets there in ~47 days. Character level progression is primarily gated by skill diversity, not by time investment in any single skill.

### 5. Combat Cap Reachability

With 12-hour windows and 1 turn/second regen:
- Turn budget per window: 43,200
- Turns per combat cycle: ~250 (200 exploration + 50 encounter cost)
- Expected combats per window: ~173
- At 50 raw XP/mob: ~8,640 raw XP
- Combat window cap: 7,000

**Players will routinely exceed the raw cap within a single window.** The efficiency system activates during every serious play session.

### 6. Guild XP Boost Interaction (Critical Finding)

The guild/shop XP boost is applied to `rawXpGain` **before** the efficiency multiplier. The boosted XP then fills the window cap at the same rate. Since the window cap is fixed, the boost **does not increase total effective XP per window**.

Simulation results (50 raw XP/action, combat window cap 7,000):

| Boost | Actions needed | Total effective XP |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 409 | 6,965 |
| 5% | 396 | 6,967 |
| 15% | 367 | 6,970 |
| 30% | 329 | 6,974 |
| 50% | 291 | 6,977 |

A **50% XP boost yields only 12 more effective XP per window** (0.17% increase). The boost's real benefit is reducing actions needed from 409 to 291 (29% fewer actions), saving turns and time.

This means guild XP boost upgrades, specializations, and shop XP boost potions are **misleadingly advertised**. A player seeing "15% XP Boost" expects 15% more XP. They actually get 15% fewer actions needed to reach the same cap, which in turn saves turns/HP/durability -- a real but non-obvious benefit.

### 7. Non-Combat vs Combat XP Asymmetry

Non-combat skills have a 2.14x higher effective daily XP rate than combat skills (20,000 vs 9,334). Yet:

- Combat skills are the primary driver of player power (damage, accuracy)
- Combat XP is harder to earn per action (requires exploration + encounter + survival)
- Non-combat XP is easier to grind (gathering: 30 turns per action, crafting: 50 turns per action, no risk)

This creates an asymmetry where support skills outpace combat skills significantly. A player who gathers and crafts all day reaches skill level 100 in ~20 days, while combat takes ~43 days.

### 8. Efficiency Curve Shape Analysis

The quadratic (power=2) efficiency curve has an important property: **87.8% of total effective XP comes from the first 70% of the window cap**. The last 30% of the cap yields only 12.2% of the window's effective XP.

| Window segment | Effective XP | Share of total |
|---|---|---|
| 0% to 70% | ~4,100 | 87.8% |
| 70% to 100% | ~567 | 12.2% |

This creates a natural "soft wall" around 70% of cap where continued grinding has severely diminished returns. Combined with the 12-hour window, this incentivizes session-based play rather than marathon grinding.

## Issues Found

### Issue 1: Guild/Shop XP Boosts Are Nearly Useless (high severity)

As demonstrated in Section 6, XP boosts applied before efficiency produce negligible increases in total effective XP per window. A 50% boost yields only +12 XP per window (+0.17%). Guild XP boost upgrades cost 5,000-20,000 treasury gold and specialization warfare tier 3 provides 15% XP boost at guild level 40 -- all for a benefit that is functionally zero in terms of total XP gained.

**Root cause:** The boost multiplies `rawXpGain`, but the efficiency function caps total output at `2/3 * windowCap` regardless. Boosted XP just fills the cap faster.

**Impact:** Players and guilds invest significant resources into XP boosts expecting meaningful XP gains. The actual benefit (fewer actions, saving turns/HP) is real but deceptive. This erodes trust in the game's systems.

### Issue 2: Marginal XP Per Level Is Too Flat at High Levels (medium severity)

The 1.8 exponent produces only a 19x increase in marginal XP from level 2 to level 100. Level 99 to 100 takes 0.76 days for combat, 0.36 days for non-combat. Max level is reachable in under 43 days per skill.

For a game designed to sustain long-term engagement, reaching max skill level within 6 weeks of dedicated play may be too fast. The "endgame" of skill leveling evaporates quickly.

**Comparison:** A 2.0 exponent would make level 99->100 take ~1.07 days (combat). A 2.2 exponent would make it ~1.5 days. A 2.5 exponent would stretch it to ~2.5 days, with level 100 taking ~85 days total.

### Issue 3: 2.14x Non-Combat vs Combat XP Gap (medium severity)

Non-combat skills reach max level 2.14x faster than combat skills. This means:
- Support characters max out their core skills weeks before combat characters
- Combat is riskier, more resource-intensive, AND slower to level
- No compensating mechanic rewards combat for its higher risk/cost

The gap exists because the daily caps are 30,000 vs 14,000, but the per-action XP and action frequency don't proportionally differ. Gathering at 30 turns/action vs combat at 250 effective turns/action means gathering generates raw XP far more efficiently per turn.

### Issue 4: Window-Based Cap Creates Degenerate Multi-Skill Rotation (low severity)

Because each skill has an independent window cap, the optimal strategy is:
1. Train combat skill A until efficiency drops below ~50%
2. Switch to combat skill B
3. Switch to combat skill C
4. Rotate through gathering, processing, crafting

This rotation maximizes total effective XP across all skills simultaneously. A player who rotates across all 15 skills earns 80,400 character XP/day vs 2,800 from single-skill focus (28.7x multiplier).

This isn't necessarily a "bug" -- it rewards diverse gameplay. But it means the efficiency system primarily punishes specialization rather than rewarding pacing.

### Issue 5: Level 1 to 10 Is Trivially Fast (low severity)

Combat skill level 10 takes 0.7 days; non-combat takes 0.3 days. The early game provides almost no sense of progression since levels fly by. Combined with 86,400 starting turns (previously 64,800), a new player can reach level 10+ in multiple skills within their first play session.

This may be intentional (avoid early-game tedium), but it compresses the "learning curve" levels into a few hours.

## Recommendations

### R1: Apply XP Boosts After Efficiency (fixes Issue 1)

**Change:** In `xpService.ts`, move the boost application to after `applyXpGain`:

```
// Current: boostedXpGain = rawXpGain * (1 + boost)  →  applyXpGain(boostedXpGain)
// Proposed: applyXpGain(rawXpGain)  →  finalXp = xpAfterEfficiency * (1 + boost)
```

**Impact:** A 15% boost would yield 15% more total effective XP per window (from 4,667 to 5,367 for combat). The window cap would remain as the unmodified baseline, and the boost would genuinely increase output. Players who invest in guild upgrades or buy boost potions would see the advertised benefit.

**Caveat:** This makes boosts more powerful, especially when stacked. A max-stacked boost (15% guild upgrade + 15% specialization + shop potion) could yield +30-50% effective XP. Consider capping total boost at 30-50% or adjusting individual source values.

### R2: Consider Raising XP_EXPONENT to 2.0 (addresses Issue 2)

**Change:** `XP_EXPONENT: 1.8` -> `2.0`

**Rationale:** At 2.0, the curve becomes quadratic:
- Level 100 total XP: 1,000,000 (vs 398,107 at 1.8)
- Level 99->100 marginal: ~19,900 (vs 7,138)
- Days to combat level 100: ~107 (vs 43)
- Days to non-combat level 100: ~50 (vs 20)

This roughly doubles time-to-max, giving the endgame more longevity. Alternatively, **keep 1.8 but reduce the daily caps** to achieve a similar effect with less disruption to existing progress.

### R3: Narrow the Combat vs Non-Combat Cap Gap (addresses Issue 3)

**Change:** `DAILY_CAP_COMBAT: 14,000` -> `20,000` (or reduce non-combat to `22,000`)

**Rationale:** A ratio of 20,000 / 30,000 = 1.5x would better reflect the risk/effort difference. Combat earns XP through risky encounters with resource costs, while gathering/crafting are safe and repetitive. A 1.5x gap (instead of 2.14x) acknowledges this without punishing combat-focused play as severely. This would bring combat to level 100 in ~30 days vs non-combat ~20 days.

### R4: Display Boost Benefit Accurately (mitigates Issue 1 if R1 is not adopted)

If the boost remains pre-efficiency, update all XP boost descriptions to read "Reduces actions needed by X%" rather than "X% XP Boost". This sets accurate player expectations. The SkillCard tooltip, guild upgrade descriptions, and shop potion descriptions all need updating.

### R5: Add a Prestige or Mastery System Beyond Level 100 (addresses Issue 2, long-term)

If the leveling curve is intentionally fast, add post-100 progression (mastery levels, prestige tiers, bonus stat unlocks) to maintain endgame engagement. This is a larger design decision but would address the concern about max level being reached in weeks.

## Risk Level

high -- The guild XP boost bug (Issue 1) means a paid/invested game mechanic delivers near-zero value for its primary advertised purpose. The efficiency system's interaction with boosts fundamentally undermines the guild upgrade and shop potion economy. Fixing the boost application order (R1) would change XP rates for all players using boosts, requiring recalibration of the efficiency curve or caps. Changing XP_EXPONENT (R2) would affect all existing player progression and would need a migration strategy for live players. Both changes touch core progression and need careful rollout.
