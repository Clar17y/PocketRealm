## Analysis

### 1. ELO System Mathematics

The ELO implementation follows the standard formula:

```
expectedA = 1 / (1 + 10^((ratingB - ratingA) / 400))
deltaA = round(K * (scoreA - expectedA))
deltaB = max(-ratingB, -deltaA)
```

With K=32 and starting rating 1000, the ELO change table for a **win** (scoreA=1):

| Rating Diff (A-B) | Expected(A) | Delta(A win) | Delta(A loss) |
|---|---|---|---|
| -400 | 0.091 | +29 | -3 |
| -200 | 0.240 | +24 | -8 |
| -100 | 0.360 | +21 | -11 |
| 0 | 0.500 | +16 | -16 |
| +100 | 0.640 | +12 | -20 |
| +200 | 0.760 | +8 | -24 |
| +400 | 0.909 | +3 | -29 |

**K=32 is the FIDE "rapid" K-factor.** This is relatively high for a persistent rating system. For context:
- FIDE uses K=40 for new players (<30 games), K=20 for established players, K=10 for 2400+ ELO
- Chess.com uses K=25-40 depending on confidence

With K=32 and a floor of 0, a player can swing up to 32 points per match. This means:
- 10 consecutive wins from equal opponents: +160 points (1000 -> 1160)
- 10 consecutive losses from equal opponents: -160 points (1000 -> 840)

**Volatility analysis:** A player at 1000 rating fighting 6 matches per day (one per cooldown cycle against the same opponent, or multiple unique opponents) can move roughly 96-192 points per day. Ratings will be noisy in a small population.

### 2. Bracket Range Asymmetry

`BRACKET_RANGE = 0.25` means the bracket window is `[rating * 0.75, rating * 1.25]`.

This creates an **asymmetric** bracket that widens as rating increases:

| Player Rating | Lower Bound | Upper Bound | Window Width |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 | 375 | 625 | 250 |
| 1000 | 750 | 1250 | 500 |
| 1500 | 1125 | 1875 | 750 |
| 2000 | 1500 | 2500 | 1000 |

The multiplicative bracket has a critical property: **it is not symmetric between two players**. Player A at 1000 sees B at 1250 (1250 <= 1250, in bracket). But player B at 1250 sees range [937, 1562], so A at 1000 is also in B's bracket. This works.

However, consider A at 800: bracket = [600, 1000]. Player B at 1000: bracket = [750, 1250]. A sees B, but B also sees A. This is fine.

The real issue emerges at low ratings. A player at 100 has bracket [75, 125] — a window of only 50 points. They can barely find opponents. Meanwhile a player at 2000 has a 1000-point window and sees almost everyone. **Low-rated players get progressively more isolated.**

At rating 0 (possible since `Math.max(0, ...)` is the floor): bracket = [0, 0]. The player is **permanently stuck** with no matchable opponents unless someone else also hits exactly 0.

### 3. Rating Floor Problem

The ELO delta for the loser includes `deltaB = Math.max(-ratingB, -deltaA)`. This prevents going below 0. But the floor of 0 combined with K=32 means:

- At rating 50, losing to a 1000-rated player costs only ~3 points (expected loss). But winning grants ~29.
- At rating 10, the maximum loss is 10 points regardless of K-factor.

This creates a "rubber band" near 0: players bounce back quickly because they can't lose much but gain normally. This is fine in isolation, but the bracket range problem (above) means they may never find opponents to fight.

### 4. Turn Economy in PvP Context

Turn costs compared to other activities:

| Activity | Turn Cost | Time Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| PvP Challenge | 500 | 8.3 minutes |
| PvP Revenge | 250 | 4.2 minutes |
| PvP Scout | 100 | 1.7 minutes |
| PvE Encounter | 50 | 50 seconds |
| Gathering | 30 | 30 seconds |
| Crafting | 50 | 50 seconds |
| Item Repair | 100 | 1.7 minutes |
| Boss Signup | 200 | 3.3 minutes |
| Training | 200 | 3.3 minutes |

**PvP challenge is the second-most expensive regular activity** (after guild creation/skill respec at 50,000). At 500 turns, one PvP fight costs the same as 10 PvE encounters.

With 64,800 turn bank cap, a player can do at most 129 challenges before running out. At 1 turn/second regen, a continuous PvP session yields one fight every 8.3 minutes — roughly 7 fights per hour.

**Scout + Challenge combo:** 100 + 500 = 600 turns. This is the "informed attack" cost. Scouting is cheap relative to the challenge (20% surcharge). The revenge discount at 250 turns (50% off) is generous enough to incentivize revenge chains.

### 5. Cooldown and Farming Analysis

With `COOLDOWN_HOURS = 6`, a player can attack the same opponent 4 times per day. Combined with K=32:

**Farming scenario:** Player A (1500) repeatedly attacks a weaker player B (1000). Even with BRACKET_RANGE, a 500-point gap means:
- A's bracket: [1125, 1875]. B at 1000 is **outside** A's bracket.
- B's bracket: [750, 1250]. A at 1500 is **outside** B's bracket.

So BRACKET_RANGE of 0.25 naturally limits the farming window to ~500 points at rating 1000. This is a reasonable safeguard.

**However**, within the bracket window, repeated attacks are possible. A at 1000 attacking B at 1100 every 6 hours:
- Each fight at ~similar rating: attacker advantage is the attacker uses real HP (risk) while defender uses max HP (ghost). The template-based system means the fight outcome is partially predictable.
- 4 attacks per day on the same person, with potential 16-20 point swings each time. That's up to 80 points/day against a single opponent.

### 6. Attacker vs Defender Asymmetry

Critical design asymmetry discovered in code:
- **Attacker uses current HP/stamina/mana** (real resources, possibly depleted from PvE)
- **Defender uses max HP/stamina/mana** (ghost, always full)

This means:
1. The attacker always fights at a disadvantage if they've been active in PvE
2. There is no penalty for the defender when attacked (no resource loss, no gold loss, only durability loss on equipment)
3. The attacker can be **knocked out** (entering recovery state, losing 30% gold, costing turns to recover)
4. The defender cannot be knocked out

**This makes PvP purely offensive-risk.** The defender suffers only ELO change and durability wear. The attacker risks ELO, turns, resources, gold, and potentially knockout recovery time.

For a player at level 30 with 5 vitality: maxHP = 100 + (5 * 5) = 125. Recovery from knockout = 125 turns. Plus 30% gold loss. Plus 500 turns already spent on the challenge. A lost PvP fight costs at minimum 625 turns + gold, and potentially more if the flee check fails.

### 7. Flee Mechanic After PvP Loss

When the attacker reaches 0 HP in PvP, the flee mechanic triggers using the **defender's character level** as `mobLevel`:

```
fleeChance = 0.3 + (evasionLevel - defenderLevel) * 0.02
```

In PvP, the bracket range means defender level is likely similar to attacker level (within 25% rating, both above level 10). Assuming similar evasion and character levels:

- Equal levels: 30% flee chance
- Outcome distribution: 70% knockout, 24% wounded escape (1 HP), 6% clean escape (15% max HP)

**This is harsh.** A PvP loss against an equal opponent results in knockout 70% of the time. Combined with the resource asymmetry (attacker at current HP, defender at max), PvP losses are very punishing.

### 8. Hit Curve Divergence

PvP uses a steeper hit curve (exponent 2.4, bias 5) versus PvE (exponent 1.5, bias 10):
- PvP min hit chance: 10% (vs 25% PvE)
- PvP bias of 5 means accuracy matters far more per point
- Exponent 2.4 means the accuracy-to-hit conversion is very steep around the crossover point

This means **accuracy and dodge stats are disproportionately powerful in PvP** compared to PvE. A player optimized for PvE (stacking damage, ignoring accuracy) will perform poorly in PvP. This is arguably intentional for strategic diversity, but the magnitude of the difference (10% floor vs 25% floor) could make PvP feel unfair for PvE-focused builds.

### 9. MIN_OPPONENTS_SHOWN Dead Constant

`MIN_OPPONENTS_SHOWN: 10` is defined but **never referenced** in any code. The ladder function (`getLadder`) returns all matching opponents within the bracket without any minimum guarantee. If fewer than 10 opponents exist in the bracket, the player simply sees fewer.

## Issues Found

### Issue 1: Rating 0 Deadlock (Severity: Medium)

A player who drops to rating 0 has a bracket of [0, 0] and cannot find any opponents. They are permanently locked out of PvP. The only escape would be someone else also reaching exactly 0.

### Issue 2: Low-Rating Bracket Starvation (Severity: Medium)

Players below ~400 rating have very narrow brackets (width <200 points). With a small player base, finding opponents becomes difficult. Combined with the 70% knockout rate on loss and 30% gold penalty, losing streaks compound into a death spiral: lose rating -> narrow bracket -> fewer opponents -> can only fight upward -> more likely to lose -> lower rating.

### Issue 3: Attacker Disadvantage is Severe (Severity: High)

The asymmetry between attacker (current resources) and defender (max resources) combined with knockout risk (70% on loss) and gold loss (30%) makes the risk/reward of initiating PvP heavily skewed. Winning gives +16 ELO. Losing costs -16 ELO plus 500+ turns plus potentially 30% gold plus recovery downtime. Rational players may avoid PvP entirely unless they can guarantee a win.

### Issue 4: K-Factor Too High for Small Population (Severity: Low-Medium)

K=32 causes significant rating volatility. In a small player base (under 100 active PvP players), ratings will oscillate wildly. A single day of 4 fights can move a player 64-128 points. This makes the leaderboard feel unstable and the bracket matching less meaningful.

### Issue 5: Unused Constant (Severity: Low)

`MIN_OPPONENTS_SHOWN` is defined but unused. It was likely intended to guarantee a minimum ladder size by widening the bracket when few opponents are found, but this logic was never implemented.

### Issue 6: Revenge Chain Exploitation (Severity: Low)

Revenge costs 250 turns (half price). Two cooperating players could alternately attack each other: Player A attacks B (500 turns), B revenges A (250 turns), A revenges B (250 turns), etc. Each subsequent fight is 250 turns. With a 6-hour cooldown, this creates a cheap ELO farming loop if one player intentionally loses. However, the cooldown on the same attacker-defender pair limits throughput, and intentional losing destroys the loser's ELO, so the practical exploit is limited.

## Recommendations

### R1: Additive Bracket Range (addresses Issues 1 & 2)

Replace multiplicative bracket with additive: `[rating - 200, rating + 200]` or hybrid `[rating * 0.8 - 50, rating * 1.2 + 50]`. This ensures a minimum 100-point window even at rating 0.

Alternatively, add a floor: `lowerBound = Math.max(0, Math.floor(rating * 0.75))` with a minimum window width of 200. This costs no behavioral change at normal ratings.

### R2: Reduce Knockout Severity After PvP (addresses Issue 3)

Options, in order of least to most disruptive:
- **Option A:** Skip the flee/knockout check entirely after PvP loss. Set attacker to 1 HP instead. Rationale: PvP already costs 500 turns; adding knockout on top is double punishment.
- **Option B:** Use a PvP-specific flee chance that's more generous (e.g., 60% base instead of 30%).
- **Option C:** Remove gold loss from PvP knockout (keep recovery turn cost).

Recommended: **Option A.** The 500-turn cost plus ELO loss is sufficient penalty. Knockout creates a chilling effect on PvP participation.

### R3: Dynamic K-Factor (addresses Issue 4)

Use a confidence-based K-factor that decreases with games played:
- Placement period (first 20 matches): K=40
- Established (20-100 matches): K=32 (current)
- Veteran (100+ matches): K=20

This reduces volatility for established players while allowing new players to find their true rating quickly.

### R4: Remove or Implement MIN_OPPONENTS_SHOWN (addresses Issue 5)

Either:
- Delete the constant if the guaranteed minimum ladder size is not desired.
- Implement bracket widening: if fewer than `MIN_OPPONENTS_SHOWN` opponents are found, progressively widen the bracket by 50-point increments until the minimum is met or a hard cap is reached.

### R5: Reduce Attacker Resource Disadvantage (addresses Issue 3)

Consider healing the attacker to a minimum HP threshold (e.g., 50% max) before PvP combat begins. This reduces the "depleted from PvE" disadvantage while still making resources meaningful (players at 80% HP still enter at 80%, not 100%).

## Risk Level

**medium** — The PvP system is functional and the ELO math is correct, but the attacker/defender asymmetry combined with knockout penalties creates a strong disincentive to participate in PvP. The bracket range issue at low ratings could strand players. These are engagement-threatening problems that won't crash anything but may cause player frustration and PvP avoidance.
