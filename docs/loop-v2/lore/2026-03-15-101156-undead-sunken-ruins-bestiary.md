# Mob Flavor Text: Undead (Sunken Ruins)

## Content

### Family Overview

The undead of the Sunken Ruins are not the same as the Haunted Marsh's mindless dead. They are older, more purposeful, and animated by something that is not the marsh's ambient necromancy but a will: a single will, sustained for centuries from the Throne Hall's dais, that has never stopped issuing orders. Where the marsh's skeletons walk on errands they cannot remember, the ruins' dead walk on errands they were never released from. They patrol in formations. They hold ranks. They obey a chain of command that was established when they were alive and has never been rescinded, because the thing that issued the orders is still issuing them.

Vesper Tain classifies the Sunken Ruins undead as "directed necromancy," a category she created specifically for this phenomenon and hopes never to need again. The marsh animates what it finds. The Lich chooses what it keeps.

---

### Drowned Sailor
*Appearance:* A waterlogged corpse in salt-crusted clothing, swollen and discoloured by centuries of submersion. The clothing is naval: rope belts, canvas jackets, and boots designed for deck work, all preserved by the saltwater into a state of permanent decay that progresses no further. The eyes are open, milky, and tracking nothing in particular. The hands move with the muscle memory of someone who spent a lifetime hauling rope and has not been told to stop.
*Behavior:* Drowned sailors stumble through the flooded entrance halls, not quite dead and not quite aware, performing fragments of duties they can no longer complete. They attack anything that disrupts their patrol, swinging waterlogged fists and improvised weapons with the mechanical repetition of something that has not received a new order in a very long time. There are no spells, no tactics, and no intelligence. There is only persistence, preserved in salt.
*Lore:* The drowned sailors are the oldest mindless undead in the Pocketrealm, predating the Haunted Marsh's skeletons by centuries. They were the lost civilisation's workforce: the ones who built, hauled, and maintained the structures that became the ruins. When the water rose, they drowned. When the Lich's will reached the entrance halls (the weakest point of its control), they stood up and resumed. They have been resuming ever since. Aldric Voss notes that their clothing predates every known textile pattern in his records. He has logged this. He does not know what to do with it.
*Combat Feel:* Tier 1 endgame trash. HP 55, defence 18, accuracy 20 are all significantly higher than equivalent Haunted Marsh undead, reflecting the tier 5 zone's baseline. No spells, no tricks; just physical damage and the durability of something that has been dead for longer than most civilisations have been alive. The fight is straightforward. The implication that you are fighting the ruins' construction crew is not.

### Skeletal Knight
*Appearance:* A skeleton in armour, which is where the similarity to the Haunted Marsh's bone patrols ends. The Skeletal Knight's armour is intact, articulated, and made from a dark metal alloy that Kessa cannot identify and Vesper suspects is Ancient Ore treated through a process that has been lost. The bones inside are white, clean, and held in formation not by sinew or magic but by the armour itself, which moves as if it remembers the body it was built for. The sword is straight, double-edged, and in better condition than anything a dead thing should be carrying.
*Behavior:* Skeletal knights patrol the mid-ruins in precise two-by-two formations, holding corridors and intersections with the mechanical discipline of a military that was professional in life and has not received orders to stand down in death. They do not speak. They do not communicate visibly. They simply maintain formation and engage anything that enters their section with the coordinated efficiency of soldiers who have had centuries to perfect a routine they can no longer choose to abandon.
*Lore:* The skeletal knights were the lost civilisation's military, and they are the strongest evidence that the civilisation had one. Their armour is uniform, standardised, mass-produced, and sized for bodies that no longer exist inside them. The formations they hold are textbook, and whoever wrote the textbook had a military philosophy that Captain Fen Darrow recognises as "uncomfortably sophisticated." He has asked Aldric to stop showing him the patrol maps. Aldric has not stopped.
*Combat Feel:* Tier 2 with high defence (22) and accuracy (21). The Skeletal Knight is a physical wall, harder to bring down than any tier 2 mob in lower zones. No spells means no magic damage, so physical defence is the only survival stat that matters. Fights are attritional: the knight does not surprise you, but it does not go down quickly either. It fights like a soldier. It was a soldier. The only thing that changed is the breathing.

### Spectral Captain
*Appearance:* A translucent figure in a uniform that glows with the cold, pale light of something that burned through its body long ago and kept burning. The uniform is military: high-collared, decorated with insignia that Aldric Voss has catalogued but cannot read (the Spectral Silk in I-14 carries the same markings), and fitted to a frame that is no longer quite physical. The Captain's face is visible, which is worse than the faceless wraiths of the Haunted Marsh: this one has features, rank, and the unmistakable expression of someone who is still, after all this time, on duty.
*Behavior:* Spectral captains lead patrols of skeletal knights through corridors they have walked for centuries, directing formations with gestures that the knights still obey. Ghost Blade on round 3 deals 10 magic damage, a focused strike of spectral energy that bypasses physical armour entirely. Spectral Chains on round 5 drops your evasion by 5 for two rounds, pinning you in place for the follow-up. The captain fights as if it has reinforcements. The belief has not changed in five hundred years.
*Lore:* The spectral captains are the Lich's intermediary officers: powerful enough to sustain their own presence, disciplined enough to maintain formations, and intelligent enough to recognise you as a threat rather than merely reacting to your warmth. They are the first undead in the Sunken Ruins that look at you with intent. The Spectral Silk woven from their dissolution carries the insignia of a rank that Aldric cannot translate and a civilisation that Vesper says was "capable of things we are only beginning to find evidence of." The Mysterious Stranger said, of the captains: "They are loyal to the last order. The last order was: hold."
*Combat Feel:* The elite. HP 80, magic defence 24, evasion 8, accuracy 23, and a two-spell rotation that combines magic burst with evasion stripping. Ghost Blade (round 3) hits hard and bypasses armour. Spectral Chains (round 5) pins you for the subsequent attacks. The captain is the bridge between the physical undead (sailors, knights) and the magical undead (the Lich), dealing magic damage but fighting with military discipline. Magic defence is essential; physical damage is the counter (defence 20 is moderate). The captain is not a wraith. It is an officer. The distinction matters.

### Lich (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Lich occupies the Throne Hall the way a thought occupies a mind: completely, invisibly, and with the quiet certainty that it was always there. It has a body, or what remains of one: a skeletal frame draped in robes that were ancient when the water rose, sitting on a throne of carved stone that the Naga Queen's dais mirrors from across the flooded hall. Its skull is intact, its eye sockets burn with a light that is not fire and not magic but something older than both, and its hands rest on the throne's arms as if it is waiting. It has been waiting for centuries. It is very good at it.
*Behavior:* The Lich fights with a four-spell rotation that is the most complex necromantic sequence in the Pocketrealm. Death Ward on round 2 buffs its defence by 5 for three rounds, because the Lich's first priority is not dying again. Necrotic Bolt on round 4 delivers 14 magic damage during the shield window, a focused blast of concentrated death that treats your health as a suggestion. Raise Dead on round 6 buffs its attack by 6 for three rounds, and the name is not metaphorical: the Lich draws power from the dead that surround it, and in the Sunken Ruins, the dead surround everything. Soul Harvest on round 8 deals 22 magic damage, the second-highest single-hit damage in the game, a direct extraction of vitality so thorough that Vesper, reading the combat logs, said: "That is not a spell. That is a thesis statement."
*Lore:* The Lich is the will that animates the Sunken Ruins' dead. It was a person once: the leader or high priest or architect of the lost civilisation (the carvings are ambiguous, and the Lich is not talking). When the civilisation fell and the water rose, the Lich chose not to follow. It bound its will to the Throne Hall and continued issuing orders to a military that could no longer disobey, because the dead do not resign. The Death Knight in the Haunted Marsh (M-16), which Vesper could not explain with environmental animation, now has an explanation: it was following orders. The orders came from here. They have not been rescinded.
*Combat Feel:* The undead family boss and one of the Pocketrealm's three endgame bosses. HP 120, defence 26, magic defence 34 (the highest in the game), accuracy 25, and a four-spell rotation that combines self-buffing, direct damage, attack amplification, and a devastating finisher. Death Ward (round 2) makes it durable. Necrotic Bolt (round 4) hits during the shield. Raise Dead (round 6) makes everything after hit harder. Soul Harvest (round 8) closes the fight if you have not closed it first. The Lich demands sustained physical damage (magic is nearly useless against 34 magic defence), enough health to absorb Soul Harvest, and the awareness that the Lich has been sitting on that throne for centuries doing nothing but perfecting this exact sequence. The Naga Queen is the most elegant boss. The Lich is the most patient. It has outlasted its civilisation, its body, and the concept of mortality itself. Outlasting you is not even interesting to it.

- **Family overview** establishes the critical distinction: Haunted Marsh undead (M-16) are environmental and mindless. Sunken Ruins undead are directed and hierarchical, animated by the Lich's sustained will. The same family, expressed through two fundamentally different mechanisms.
- **"Directed necromancy"**: Vesper's new category, created specifically for the Lich's control. This distinguishes the ruins' undead from every other undead in the Pocketrealm and establishes the Lich as the only known intentional necromancer.
- **Death Knight resolution:** The Haunted Marsh's Death Knight (M-16), which Vesper could not explain with environmental animation and the Stranger described as serving "something that does not have a name yet," now has an explanation: it was following the Lich's orders. The orders reached the marsh from the ruins. This closes the Death Knight lore loop opened in M-16 and connects the two undead populations across zones.
- **Cross-references:**
  - Vesper Tain (N-04) classifies the phenomenon ("directed necromancy"), suspects Ancient Ore armour on the knights, assesses the Spectral Captains' civilisation, and delivers the Lich's definitive one-liner: "That is not a spell. That is a thesis statement."
  - Aldric Voss (N-05) catalogues what he cannot read: the drowned sailors' textile patterns, the spectral captains' insignia. His compulsive archiving of incomprehensible data continues from the Ancient Relics (I-14).

- **Undead family site nouns** (Grave, Crypt, Tomb) map to the ruins' progression: sailors in flooded graves (entrance halls), knights in crypts (mid-ruins corridors), the Lich in its tomb (the Throne Hall).
- **Lich as "most patient" boss:** Positioned against the Naga Queen (most elegant) and the Eldritch Abomination (upcoming, most alien). Three endgame bosses, three design philosophies: surgical rotation, sustained attrition, and (for the Abomination) something else entirely. The Lich's magic defence 34 is the highest in the game, making it the hardest boss to damage with spells but also the most straightforward to strategise against: physical damage, health potions, persistence. The Lich rewards preparation. It has had centuries of it.
