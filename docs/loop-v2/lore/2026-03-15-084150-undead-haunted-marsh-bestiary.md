# Mob Flavor Text: Undead (Haunted Marsh)

## Content

### Family Overview

The dead of the Haunted Marsh were people once. Some of them still look like it. The marsh does not create undead so much as refuse to let things stay dead: the water preserves, the mud holds, and whatever ancient magic seeps up from beneath the peat animates what it finds with the indiscriminate enthusiasm of a process that has forgotten how to stop. They are not controlled. They are not organised. They simply persist, moving through the mist on errands they can no longer remember, attacking anything that still has the warmth they lost.

Vesper Tain has studied the phenomenon from a safe distance and concluded that the animation is environmental rather than intentional. The marsh itself is the necromancer. Whatever died here stays, and whatever stays eventually walks. The only exception is the Death Knight, who moves with purpose the others lack. Vesper does not have an explanation for the Death Knight. She has a concerned expression and a request for more data.

---

### Skeleton
*Appearance:* Yellowed bone held together by sinew that should have rotted centuries ago and a faint, greenish luminescence that clings to the joints like phosphorescence. The skull is usually missing the jaw, which does not stop it from trying to bite. Its weapons, if it carries them, are corroded relics of whatever it held when it died: a sword, a tool, a walking stick. The skeleton does not choose its equipment. It uses what it wakes up with.
*Behavior:* Skeletons rise from the mud of the Bonefields with the unhurried patience of something that has all the time in the world and no concept of urgency. They walk in straight lines toward anything living, swinging whatever they are holding. There is no strategy, no coordination, and no retreat. A skeleton does not know it can be defeated. It does not know anything. It simply continues until it cannot.
*Lore:* The bones surface through the marsh's mud in cycles, like geological deposits that happen to be armed. Gavrik Stoneshoulder's guild contracts for the Bonefields include a standard caveat: "Count will vary. Terrain provides." He means that the number of skeletons in any given stretch depends on what the mud feels like giving up that day. The Bone Fragment (I-13) from their remains carries a residual chill that weaponsmiths have learned to use rather than explain.
*Combat Feel:* Moderate defence (16) and accuracy (17), very low evasion (3) and magic defence (8). Skeletons are the marsh's introduction: durable enough to demand respect, slow enough to manage, and trivially weak to magic. They fight like furniture that learned to hold a weapon. The threat is not any single skeleton. The threat is that there are always more.

### Zombie
*Appearance:* A waterlogged corpse in various stages of decomposition, swollen and discoloured by the marsh's brackish immersion. What remains of its clothing is unidentifiable. What remains of its face is worse. It moves with a lurching, mechanical gait, the motion of muscles that have forgotten the sequence but whose body refuses to acknowledge the error. The smell arrives before the zombie does, which is the only warning you get.
*Behavior:* Zombies are slower than skeletons but considerably more durable, absorbing damage with the indifference of something that does not process pain and has no particular attachment to the integrity of its own limbs. They swing with their fists, their jaws, or whatever part of themselves connects first. Precision is not a concept they have access to. Volume is.
*Lore:* The marsh's zombies are the most recent dead: adventurers, travellers, and (Captain Fen Darrow suspects, though he does not say it publicly) missing members of Thornwall's eastern patrols. The armour scraps and guild insignia occasionally visible on the fresher specimens are politely not discussed. The memorial wall in Thornwall grows longer. The marsh grows fuller. Nobody draws the connection out loud.
*Combat Feel:* High HP (55), low accuracy (15), almost no evasion (1). The zombie is a damage sponge that hits inconsistently but can roll high (4-10 range means occasional spikes). Fights are attrition battles against something that does not tire, does not flinch, and does not understand that it has already lost. Magic damage bypasses their negligible magic defence (6) and ends the tedium quickly.

### Wraith
*Appearance:* A translucent figure of pale light and darker absence, drifting through the marsh mist as if the mist were its element and the world were the intrusion. It has the outline of a person but the details have dissolved into something less specific: a face without features, hands without fingers, a presence without a body. The temperature drops when a wraith approaches. The mist thickens. Your breath becomes visible, and the wraith watches it with something that is not curiosity and not hunger but occupies the space between both.
*Behavior:* Wraiths do not walk. They drift, materialising from the mist without warning and engaging with the focused malice of something that remembers being alive and resents anything that still is. Life Drain on round 3 deals 8 magic damage and heals the wraith for the same amount, a transfer of vitality so direct that you can feel it leave. Fear on round 5 drops your attack by 4 for two rounds, flooding your mind with the cold, drowning certainty of your own eventual end. Between spells, it deals magic damage from a distance that physical weapons struggle to close.
*Lore:* Wraiths are what remains when the marsh's preservation fails to keep the body but succeeds in keeping the will. They are the angriest dead, the ones who died badly enough that the manner of their death became their entire personality. Vesper Tain classifies them as "concentrated necromantic residue" and says they are the strongest evidence that the marsh's animation is environmental, because nothing intentional would create something this hostile and this unhappy. The Wraith Essence (I-13) collected from their dissolution point is as cold as the wraith that produced it.
*Combat Feel:* The marsh's first elite threat. High evasion (10), high magic defence (20), magic damage type, and a two-spell rotation that creates compounding pressure. Life Drain (round 3) heals the wraith while hurting you, extending the fight beyond what the HP pool suggests. Fear (round 5) cripples your damage output when you need it most. Physical defence is low (14), so sustained physical hits work, but you need accuracy to connect through that evasion and enough health to survive the drain. The wraith is a test of whether your build can handle something that does not play by physical rules.

### Death Knight (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Death Knight is not a skeleton with armour. It is an armoured presence that happens to contain bones. Full plate, blackened and corroded but intact, sealed at every joint as if the armour itself is what holds the occupant together. The helmet visor is down, and behind it there is nothing but a faint, cold light that tracks your movement with the precision of something that was, in life, very good at killing and has lost none of the skill. The armour screams faintly as it moves, metal grinding against metal in a sound that is not rust and is not wind. Gavrik heard it from the Bonefields' edge and did not go closer. It is the sound that cost him his hand the first time he ignored it.
*Behavior:* The Death Knight fights with military discipline: deliberate, sequential, and escalating. Dark Aura on round 2 buffs its own attack by 5 for three rounds, wreathing it in a nimbus of cold, dark energy that makes the mist recoil. Soul Strike on round 4 delivers 12 magic damage during the buff window, a focused blast of necrotic force that bypasses physical armour entirely. Death Blow on round 7 is the executioner's swing: 20 physical damage from a greatsword that has been killing for longer than most adventurers have been alive. The Death Knight does not retreat. It does not posture. It finishes what it starts, and it starts with the assumption that you are already dead and simply have not noticed.
*Lore:* The Death Knight is the one undead in the Haunted Marsh that Vesper cannot explain with environmental animation. It moves with intent. It fights with technique. It patrols a route through the Bonefields that has not changed in the years since Thornwall's scouts first mapped it, as if it is still following orders from a command structure that no longer exists. The Mysterious Stranger, on the subject of the Death Knight, said: "It was a soldier. It is still a soldier. The only thing that changed is what it serves, and what it serves does not have a name yet." Captain Fen Darrow heard this secondhand and did not sleep well for a week.
*Combat Feel:* The Haunted Marsh boss. HP 100, defence 24, accuracy 21, and a three-spell rotation that combines self-buffing, magic damage, and a devastating physical finisher. Dark Aura (round 2) creates a damage amplification window. Soul Strike (round 4) bypasses armour during that window. Death Blow (round 7) punishes anyone still standing with raw physical force. The Death Knight demands both physical and magical defence, sustained damage output, and the awareness that this is not a mindless skeleton. This is something that remembers how to fight and has had eternity to practice.

## Integration Notes

- Store as `bestiaryFlavorText` on the `MobTemplate` model, same pattern as all bestiary entries.
- **Family overview** establishes that Haunted Marsh undead are environmentally animated (the marsh is the necromancer), not controlled by any intelligence. This is a deliberate contrast with the Sunken Ruins undead (M-21 upcoming), who serve a purpose and follow ancient hierarchies. The distinction makes the same family feel different across zones.
- **Two populations, one family:** Like wolves (M-04/M-14) and bandits (M-05/M-15), the undead appear in two zones. The Haunted Marsh undead are recent, mindless, and environmental. The Sunken Ruins undead (upcoming) are ancient, purposeful, and tied to the lost civilisation. The Death Knight bridges the two: it has the Marsh's physical presence but the Ruins' sense of purpose.
- **Cross-references:**
  - Vesper Tain (N-04) anchors the scientific perspective: "environmental animation," "concentrated necromantic residue," and her inability to explain the Death Knight. This continues her pattern of clinical comfort with disturbing things (I-06, I-13, I-14) while introducing the first phenomenon that genuinely concerns her.
  - Gavrik Stoneshoulder (N-08) referenced in the skeleton (guild contracts, "terrain provides") and Death Knight entries (the sound that cost him his hand, first established in Z-09's Bonefields). This makes the undead personal through Gavrik's physical cost.
  - Captain Fen Darrow (N-11) referenced in the zombie entry: the unspoken suspicion that some zombies are former patrol members. The Thornwall memorial wall (Z-09) grows because the marsh grows. This adds quiet tragedy to what is otherwise a damage sponge encounter.
  - The Mysterious Stranger (N-12) delivers a lore hook in the Death Knight entry: "what it serves does not have a name yet." This connects to the Stranger's pattern of revealing larger truths (Thornwall's true purpose in N-12, the Grove-Ruins connection) and implies the Death Knight serves something connected to the Sunken Ruins.
  - Wraith Essence (I-13) and Bone Fragment (I-13) are referenced, connecting the bestiary to the materials economy.
- **Undead family site nouns** (Grave, Crypt, Tomb) map to the Bonefields terrain: skeletons in graves, zombies in crypts (waterlogged burial chambers), the Death Knight in its tomb (the deepest Bonefields).
- **Design theme:** The Haunted Marsh undead occupy the "attrition and magic" niche. Skeletons are durable physical targets weak to magic. Zombies are HP sponges. Wraiths are magic-damage dealers with sustain. The Death Knight combines all three: physical durability, magic offence, and self-buffing. The family's design arc is: "physical rules stop working, and you need to adapt."
- **Tonal register:** The undead entries are the first bestiary category where the humor carries genuine sadness. The zombies wearing patrol insignia, the memorial wall, Fen Darrow's silence. The monsters are people. The writing does not let you forget that.
