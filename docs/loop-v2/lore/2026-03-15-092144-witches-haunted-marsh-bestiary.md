# Mob Flavor Text: Witches (Haunted Marsh)

## Content

### Family Overview

The witches of the Haunted Marsh are not the marsh's creation. They are its tenants. Where the undead are animated by ambient necromancy and the swamp beasts are adapted biology, the witches chose to be here: they came to the marsh for its magic, built their hollows in its deepest reaches, and practice their craft with the focused intent of people who consider civilisation a distraction and morality a suggestion. They are the only intelligent, organised threat in the marsh, and they are the reason Vesper Tain will study the marsh's specimens but refuses to enter it personally.

The Coven Hollows (Z-09) are their territory: sunken clearings marked by thorn hedges and bone chimes, difficult to find and more difficult to leave. The marsh's other inhabitants give the hollows a wide berth. Even the undead avoid the witches' territory, which is noteworthy, because the undead avoid nothing.

---

### Hag Servant
*Appearance:* Stooped, robed in layers of marsh-stained cloth, with a face obscured by a low hood and hands that glow faintly with greenish light. Whether the hag servant is a person who became this or was always this is a question that Vesper has asked and the witches have not answered. The glow in the hands is not decorative. It is the warmup.
*Behavior:* Hag servants patrol the edges of the Coven Hollows, casting from behind thorn screens and retreating when pressed. Curse on round 3 drops your defence by 3 for two rounds, stripping your armour's effectiveness at the exact moment the servant's allies close in. They deal magic damage with every hit, which means physical armour is already less useful before the Curse makes it worse. Hag servants do not fight to kill. They fight to weaken, and what comes after the weakening is the point.
*Lore:* The relationship between hag servants and the coven is unclear. They may be apprentices. They may be victims. The distinction, in the Coven Hollows, is philosophical rather than practical: they serve, they cast, and they do not leave. Gavrik's guild contracts list them as "hostile caster, tier 1" and do not speculate further.
*Combat Feel:* Low HP (35) and defence (10) but meaningful magic defence (14), evasion (6), and a debuff that strips your defences. The hag servant is a glass support: fragile but dangerous if ignored, because that Curse compounds with everything else the marsh throws at you. Kill them first, or fight everything that follows at a disadvantage.

### Cursed Villager
*Appearance:* A person. That is the worst part. The Cursed Villager is recognisably human: tattered clothing, calloused hands, a face that was ordinary before something was done to it. The eyes are wrong, though. Milky, unfocused, and tracking you with a precision that the blank expression does not match. They move with the jerky urgency of someone trying to fight their own body and losing.
*Behavior:* Cursed villagers attack with desperate, uncoordinated violence: fists, improvised weapons, whatever their hands find. The strength behind it is more than a person that size should have, because the curse sustains what the body cannot. No spells. No magic. Just physical damage and the awful durability of someone who no longer feels pain. They do not speak. They make sounds that used to be words.
*Lore:* Nobody knows where the cursed villagers come from. Thornwall has no record of a settlement in the marsh, and Millbrook has no missing persons reports that match. Captain Fen Darrow has his suspicions: travellers who wandered too deep, patrol members who never returned, people the marsh took and the witches kept. The fact that cursed villagers are classified under "witches" rather than "undead" is a distinction that matters to Aldric's filing system and to nobody else. They are alive. They should not be.
*Combat Feel:* The witch family's only physical threat. No spells, no magic damage, but HP 45 and defence 14 make them durable for tier 1, and the damage range (4-9) can spike. Fighting a cursed villager is mechanically straightforward and emotionally unpleasant. The bestiary exists to make you understand what you are hitting.

### Bog Witch
*Appearance:* Tall, gaunt, and dressed in the Witch Cloth (I-13) that Vesper cannot identify: dark fabric that repels water, resists fire, and hums at frequencies that make nearby potions bubble. Her face is painted with symbols in a pigment that glows faintly in the mist, and her staff is bogwood crowned with a crystal that pulses in rhythm with her heartbeat (or something's heartbeat; the two may not be the same). She moves through the marsh without disturbing the water, which is impossible, and she does it anyway.
*Behavior:* Bog witches are the coven's practitioners: the ones who do the actual magic, set the wards, weave the curses, and maintain the Hollows. Hex on round 2 drops your accuracy by 3 for two rounds, making you miss when you need to connect. Shadow Bolt on round 4 delivers 9 magic damage while you are still debuffed. Drain Life on round 6 deals 7 magic damage and heals 7, extending a fight you needed to end two rounds ago. The rotation is a machine: debuff, punish, sustain. It does not vary. It does not need to.
*Lore:* The bog witches weave the Witch Cloth that adventurers covet and Vesper cannot reverse-engineer. They brew things in the Hollows that produce acrid smoke visible from a quarter-mile away. Their magic is old, older than the marsh's necromancy, and Vesper believes it may be the reason the marsh is necromantic at all: the witches did not come to the marsh for its magic. The magic came because of them. This theory is unsupported by evidence and supported by the uncomfortable fact that the Coven Hollows are the marsh's oldest structures.
*Combat Feel:* The elite caster. HP 55, magic defence 18, evasion 8, and a three-spell rotation that creates sustained, compounding pressure. Hex (round 2) cripples accuracy. Shadow Bolt (round 4) delivers guaranteed damage during the debuff window. Drain Life (round 6) heals the witch while hurting you. The bog witch is the inverse of the swamp beasts: where those fights test raw damage output against a wall, this fight tests your ability to survive magic pressure while your accuracy is compromised. Physical damage works (defence is only 12), but you have to land the hits.

### Coven Mother (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Coven Mother does not look like her servants. She looks like what they are trying to become. Tall, angular, with skin that has the grey-green tint of the marsh itself and eyes that glow with a cold, steady light that does not flicker. Her robes are layered Witch Cloth, and the symbols on her face and hands are not paint: they are part of her, as if the magic she practices has become indistinguishable from her body. She sits at the centre of the deepest Hollow on a throne of woven thorn, and the marsh bends around her. The mist parts when she moves. The water stills. The bone chimes fall silent. Everything in the Coven Hollows knows who is in charge, including the Hollows themselves.
*Behavior:* The Coven Mother fights with a four-spell rotation, the most complex casting pattern in the Haunted Marsh. Dark Shield on round 2 buffs her defence by 5 for three rounds, making her harder to kill during setup. Poison Cloud on round 4 delivers 10 magic damage during the shield window. Curse of Weakness on round 6 drops your defence by 4 for two rounds, stripping your survivability at the moment you most need it. Cataclysm on round 8 delivers 18 magic damage to whatever is left of you. The pattern is surgical: protect herself, damage you, weaken you, finish you. Every round has a purpose, and the purpose is your end.
*Lore:* The Coven Mother is the oldest and most powerful witch in the Haunted Marsh, and possibly the oldest living human in the Pocketrealm ("living" is used loosely). The Mysterious Stranger, who speaks of the Death Knight's unknown master and the Sunken Ruins' lost civilisation with detached curiosity, speaks of the Coven Mother with something closer to respect: "She learned the marsh's language before anyone else thought to listen. What she heard, she kept. What she kept, she became." Vesper Tain has requested samples from the Coven Mother's workstation. The request has been declined, nonverbally, in ways that Vesper does not discuss.
*Combat Feel:* The witch family boss. HP 85, magic defence 24, accuracy 20, and a four-spell rotation that is the longest and most punishing caster sequence in the zone. Dark Shield (round 2) forces you to burn through extra defence during rounds 2-4. Poison Cloud (round 4) deals guaranteed magic damage. Curse of Weakness (round 6) strips your defence before the finisher. Cataclysm (round 8) delivers 18 damage to whatever remains. The Coven Mother tests everything: sustained damage to break shields, magic defence to survive the spells, enough health to reach round 8 and beyond. The Death Knight tests your gear. The Ancient Crocodile tests your patience. The Coven Mother tests whether you can survive someone who is smarter than you.

- **The three Haunted Marsh families form a complete design triad:**
  - Undead: attrition and magic damage (wear you down)
  - Swamp Beasts: physical durability (wall you out)
  - Witches: debuffs and magic pressure (dismantle you)
