# Mob Flavor Text: Abominations (Sunken Ruins)

## Content

### Family Overview

The abominations of the Sunken Ruins are not creatures in any useful sense of the word. They are what lives at the bottom: in the warm water, in the soft stone, in the darkness that was here before the civilisation built above it and that is here now that the civilisation is gone. The Lich commands the dead. The Naga Queen commands the water. The abominations command nothing. They are not intelligent enough for command and not simple enough for instinct. They occupy a space between, where biology becomes suggestion and anatomy becomes negotiable, and they have been there for longer than the ruins have been ruins.

Vesper Tain has studied every specimen brought back from the Sunken Ruins. She has classified the oozes (reluctantly), the tentacle horrors (with difficulty), and the flesh golems (with visible discomfort). The Eldritch Abomination she has not classified. She studied an Eldritch Fragment for three days, returned it without comment, and has not asked for another. When pressed, she said only: "Some things are older than names. Naming them does not make them smaller."

---

### Ooze
*Appearance:* A translucent, viscous mass approximately the size of a large barrel, clinging to the walls and ceilings of the flooded entrance halls with a grip that defies gravity and good sense. It has no eyes, no mouth, and no discernible anatomy. It has surface tension, and the surface tension is acidic. The Ooze Residue (I-14) it leaves behind dissolves everything except glass, which Vesper considers "chemically admirable."
*Behavior:* Oozes are the ruins' simplest abomination: they cling, they dissolve, they move toward warmth. Acid Splash on round 3 deals 6 damage as a spray of corrosive fluid that eats through armour and flesh with equal disinterest. Between spells, the ooze attacks through direct contact, which is exactly as unpleasant as it sounds. It does not dodge (evasion 0). It does not retreat. It does not do anything that requires a nervous system, because it does not have one.
*Lore:* Oozes are the most common abomination and the only one that Vesper can explain with conventional biology (barely). She classifies them as "aggressive colonial organisms," meaning they are not one creature but many, functioning as a collective that has decided dissolving things is its purpose. They have been in the ruins for as long as anyone has explored them, which is not long, because the exploration tends to end when the ooze notices you.
*Combat Feel:* Tier 1 with zero evasion and high defence (20). The ooze is a bag of HP that you cannot miss and that takes longer to kill than you expect. Acid Splash on round 3 adds a damage spike. The fight is a pure damage check: can you kill it before the acid damage accumulates? Magic attacks target its weak magic defence (8), making casters the natural counter to something that dissolves physical objects for a living.

### Tentacle Horror
*Appearance:* A mass of dark, muscular limbs extending from a body (if it has a body) concealed within a flooded doorway, a submerged alcove, or any opening large enough to hide something that does not want to be seen in its entirety. The tentacles are smooth, boneless, and lined with suckers that leave circular welts on anything they grip. How many tentacles the horror has is a question that depends on how many you have seen, and the answer is always "not all of them."
*Behavior:* Tentacle horrors reach from concealment, striking at anything that passes through their section of corridor. Grapple on round 3 seizes you and drops your evasion by 4 for two rounds, holding you in place while the limbs constrict. Between grapples, it strikes with whichever tentacles are free, and something always is. The horror does not pursue. It does not need to. It is already where you need to go, and it occupies the doorway with the certainty of something that knows you cannot go around.
*Lore:* Nobody has seen a tentacle horror's full body. Vesper has a theory that there is no body; that the tentacles are independent organisms sharing a common root system, like a fungus with ambition. Aldric Voss has filed the tentacle horror under "invertebrate, hostile, do not approach from below," which is the longest classification in his bestiary and the only one that includes navigational advice.
*Combat Feel:* Tier 2 debuffer. HP 50, accuracy 20, moderate evasion (4), and Grapple (round 3) strips your evasion while the horror's physical damage accumulates. The fight is about breaking free before the damage compounds: sustained physical output works (defence 16 is modest), but the evasion penalty means everything the horror throws lands harder during the grapple window.

### Flesh Golem
*Appearance:* A lurching assemblage of parts that were never meant to be together, held in a roughly humanoid shape by sutures of sinew and a biology that has abandoned coherence in favour of mass. It stands seven feet tall, hunched under the weight of its own construction, and each limb is a different size, colour, and (based on the joints) species. Its face, if the front of its head qualifies, is a flat expanse of skin stretched over something that might be a skull if skulls came in that shape. It moves through the water with the grinding determination of something that does not know what it is and does not care.
*Behavior:* Flesh golems are the abomination family's heavy: slow, massive, and built to absorb punishment and return it. Slam on round 3 delivers 10 physical damage with a limb that has the mass of a small tree trunk and the precision of a landslide. Regenerate on round 6 heals 10 HP, because the flesh golem's biology, such as it is, repairs damage by repurposing whatever tissue is closest. The repair is visible. It is not something you want to watch. Zero evasion. Nowhere to dodge. It stands, it hits, it heals, it continues.
*Lore:* The flesh golems were not built by the Lich (its necromancy animates the dead, not the reassembled). They were not built by the naga (the naga find them distasteful, which is saying something). They appear to have assembled themselves from the ruins' abundant supply of biological material, driven by an imperative that is not intelligence and not instinct but something more fundamental: the deep, dumb persistence of matter that has learned to move and has not learned to stop. Vesper calls them "the worst thing about the Sunken Ruins," which, given her competition includes oozes, wraiths, and a lich, is a statement of considerable weight.
*Combat Feel:* The elite. HP 90 (highest non-boss in the ruins), defence 24, accuracy 22, and a Slam/Regenerate rotation that creates a durability wall. Slam (round 3) hits hard. Regenerate (round 6) heals hard. The flesh golem is the Sunken Ruins' version of the Swamp Hydra: burst it before the healing cycle gives back what you took, or lose a war of attrition against something that repairs itself from the inside. Magic defence (8) is the weak point. The golem's body is physical. Its vulnerabilities are arcane.

### Eldritch Abomination (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Eldritch Abomination does not have an appearance. It has an impression, and the impression is wrong. It occupies the deepest chamber of the Sunken Ruins, where the water is warm and the stone is soft and the bioluminescence does not reach, and what you see when you enter is a suggestion of mass, of movement, of geometry that does not resolve into any shape your mind is equipped to process. There are limbs, or extensions, or projections. They move. The central body (if it is a body) pulses with a light that does not illuminate (the same light as the Eldritch Fragment in I-14). It is enormous. It is patient. It has been here since before the ruins were built, since before the civilisation arrived, since before the stone was stone. The Sunken Ruins were not built above it. They were built around it. Everything else in this place is a tenant. The Abomination is the landlord.
*Behavior:* The Eldritch Abomination fights with a four-spell rotation that is the most punishing sequence in the Pocketrealm. Madness Aura on round 2 drops your accuracy by 5 for three rounds, the single most devastating debuff in the game: not a tactical inconvenience but a fundamental impairment, as the Abomination's proximity makes your perception unreliable in ways that have nothing to do with your eyes. Void Bolt on round 4 delivers 14 magic damage while you are still debuffed and missing. Tentacle Storm on round 6 deals 18 magic damage across a wave of extensions that fill the chamber and leave no safe angle. Consume on round 9 deals 28 magic damage, the single highest damage in the entire game, as the Abomination reaches for you with something that is not a limb and takes something that is not just health. Vesper, reading the report of the first adventurer to survive Consume, described the spell as "a transaction, not an attack," and refused to elaborate.
*Lore:* The Eldritch Abomination is the oldest thing in the Sunken Ruins, and possibly the oldest thing in the Pocketrealm. The lost civilisation built around it. The carvings in the Throne Hall (the ones Aldric files under "do not open") depict something beneath the city that the civilisation worshipped, feared, or both simultaneously, which is the same thing in a dark enough context. The Lich ignores it. The Naga Queen avoids it. The Mysterious Stranger, who speaks of the Lich with patience, the Queen with respect, and the Coven Mother with something approaching deference, speaks of the Eldritch Abomination with silence. One silence. Once. Then changed the subject.
*Combat Feel:* The hardest boss in the Pocketrealm. HP 140 (the highest in the game), defence 28, magic defence 32, accuracy 24, base damage 10-20 (the highest), and a four-spell rotation that combines the most crippling debuff, sustained magic damage, and the game's ultimate finisher. Madness Aura (round 2) guts your accuracy for three rounds, turning the early fight into a scramble of missed attacks and wasted turns. Void Bolt (round 4) and Tentacle Storm (round 6) deal escalating magic damage during and after the debuff window. Consume (round 9) at 28 damage is not a spell; it is a statement that the fight is over if you have not already won. The Naga Queen is elegant. The Lich is patient. The Eldritch Abomination is inevitable. It does not care about your strategy. It cares about whether you can survive long enough for your strategy to matter, and 140 HP of magic-shielded, debuff-laden, 28-damage-finale says that most strategies will not.
