# Mob Flavor Text: Bandits (Whispering Plains)

## Content

### Family Overview

The bandits of the Whispering Plains are not the same operation as the Deep Forest's toll-collectors. They are worse. Where the forest bandits hide in the trees and prefer intimidation over bloodshed, the plains bandits have abandoned subtlety entirely. They control the trade routes between Thornwall and the eastern settlements, operating from fortified ravine camps with sentries, supply lines, and a command structure that Captain Fen Darrow describes as "disturbingly competent." The forest bandits are criminals. The plains bandits are an army that has not yet decided what it wants to be when it grows up.

The connection between the two groups is unclear. Lira Caravel suspects the plains operation was founded by forest bandits who outgrew the trees and needed more room to work. The plains offer exactly that: open sightlines, chokepoints at every ravine crossing, and trade caravans that have to pass through whether they like it or not.

---

### Highway Bandit
*Appearance:* Dressed in dusty leather and sun-bleached cloth, with a scarf pulled over the nose and a short blade at the hip. Leaner and more weathered than their forest counterparts, shaped by wind, heat, and the long stretches of nothing between targets. They look like someone who has been waiting, and is very good at it.
*Behavior:* Highway bandits patrol the trade routes in pairs, watching for caravans and lone travellers from the ridgeline. They step out of the grass with weapons drawn and an offer: your goods or your health. Most people choose their goods. The ones who choose their health discover that highway bandits fight with the flat, efficient competence of people who have done this hundreds of times and intend to do it hundreds more.
*Lore:* Lira Caravel's supply manifests include a line item for "route attrition" that she adjusts quarterly based on bandit activity. The number has been climbing. She does not complain about this publicly, because complaining implies she has not already priced it in, and Lira always prices it in.
*Combat Feel:* Balanced tier 1 entry. HP 32, accuracy 14, defence 10. Slightly sturdier than the forest's Woodland Bandit, reflecting the harsh conditions of open-ground life. A straightforward fight that introduces the plains bandit family without surprises.

### Bandit Archer
*Appearance:* Lighter gear than the highway bandit, built for mobility and range: a short recurve bow, a quiver of black-fletched arrows, and soft-soled boots that make no sound on sandstone. Positioned on the ridges above the trade routes, where the elevation turns accuracy into inevitability.
*Behavior:* Bandit archers hold the high ground. This is not a preference; it is doctrine. They fire from sandstone ridges and ravine lips, relocating between volleys with the unhurried confidence of someone who knows you have to climb twenty feet of loose rock before you can reach them. In close quarters they are fragile. Getting to close quarters is the problem.
*Lore:* The Whispering Plains' constant wind makes archery a specialist skill. The bandit archers have learned to read the grass for wind direction, timing their shots to the lulls between gusts. Rowan Delk, who reads the same wind for gathering routes, says the archers' technique is "excellent, if you can ignore what they are pointing it at."
*Combat Feel:* Glass cannon. Highest accuracy (15) and damage in the trash tier, lowest HP (26) and defence. The ranged theme is narrative rather than mechanical (all combat is resolved the same way), but the description explains why accuracy is high: the archer has the high ground and the wind at her back. Close the fight quickly before the damage accumulates.

### Bandit Lieutenant
*Appearance:* Better equipped than the rank and file: reinforced leather armour, a proper longsword (not the short blades the highway bandits carry), and a shield strapped to the left arm. A map case hangs from the belt, because the Lieutenant's job is as much planning as fighting. The expression is calm, watchful, and entirely unimpressed by you.
*Behavior:* The Bandit Lieutenant commands the fortified ravine camps, organizing patrols, managing supply caches, and handling the kind of logistics that turn a band of thieves into a functioning military unit. Dirty Trick on round 3 is exactly what it says: a thrown fistful of sand, a kicked rock, a low blow, whatever it takes to drop your accuracy by 3 for two rounds while the Lieutenant presses the advantage. Honour is a luxury the Lieutenant traded for results a long time ago.
*Lore:* Gavrik Stoneshoulder's guild contracts reference the Lieutenant by rank rather than name, because there are several of them and they rotate camps on a schedule that suggests someone with military training set it up. Captain Fen Darrow has a theory about which former regiment supplied the plains bandits' officer corps. He has not shared it, because the theory implicates people he used to serve with.
*Combat Feel:* The elite tier. HP 50, defence 14, accuracy 16, and Dirty Trick (round 3) creates a two-round window where your accuracy drops, making every miss cost more against a durable target. The Lieutenant is the first plains bandit who fights intelligently: debuff, then exploit. Cleansing potions or high base accuracy counter the trick; lacking either means a longer, costlier fight.

### Bandit Warlord (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Bandit Warlord does not look like a bandit. He looks like a general who lost his army and built a new one from whatever was available. Full plate armour, scavenged and repaired but maintained to campaign standard. A two-handed war hammer, because swords are for duels and the Warlord has no interest in duels. His face is scarred, sun-darkened, and carries the expression of a man who has been underestimated so many times that he no longer considers it an insult. He considers it an advantage.
*Behavior:* The Bandit Warlord commands from the largest entrenched position on the plains: a sandstone ravine fortified with wooden palisades, watchfires, and enough supplies to outlast a siege. Battle Cry on round 2 buffs his attack by 4 for three rounds (the same mechanic as the Pack Alpha's Howl, because leadership sounds different but functions the same). Shield Bash on round 4 delivers 8 damage and interrupts momentum. Devastating Blow on round 6 is the finisher: 14 damage from the war hammer, delivered with the full weight of a man who has been fighting since before Thornwall had walls.
*Lore:* The Warlord is the reason the plains bandits are an army rather than a rabble. He appeared on the plains three years ago with a core of disciplined fighters and absorbed every scattered bandit group between Thornwall and the eastern routes into a single operation. Lira Caravel considers him a business problem. Captain Fen Darrow considers him a military one. The Mysterious Stranger, in one of their rarer appearances, said only: "He is building something. Whether he finishes it depends on people like you." Nobody found that comforting.
*Combat Feel:* The plains bandit boss. HP 75, defence 18, accuracy 18, and a three-spell rotation that escalates pressure. Battle Cry (round 2) mirrors the Pack Alpha's Howl: a buff window that makes every subsequent hit harder. Shield Bash (round 4) disrupts your rhythm. Devastating Blow (round 6) punishes fights that drag. The Warlord fights like a professional soldier, not a bandit, and the stats reflect it. Where the Bandit Captain in the Deep Forest was a mysterious leader, the Warlord is a known quantity, which is worse. You know exactly what he can do. That does not help.

## Integration Notes

- Store as `bestiaryFlavorText` on the `MobTemplate` model, same pattern as all bestiary entries.
- **Family overview** establishes the plains bandits as a distinct, militarized operation separate from (but possibly descended from) the Deep Forest bandits (M-05). This is the second mob family (after wolves, M-04/M-14) that appears in two zones with different identities: forest bandits are covert criminals, plains bandits are an overt military force.
- **Differentiation from M-05:** The Deep Forest bandits are ambush thieves who hide in trees and prefer intimidation. The plains bandits are a fortified military operation that controls trade routes openly. The escalation is deliberate: players who fought the Bandit Captain in the forest will recognise the same family but at a higher level of organisation and threat.
- **Cross-references:**
  - Captain Fen Darrow (N-11) anchors the military perspective: he describes the bandits as "disturbingly competent" and has an uncomfortable theory about their officer corps being former soldiers from his own regiment. This deepens both Fen Darrow's character and the Warlord's backstory.
  - Lira Caravel (N-09) anchors the economic perspective: "route attrition" line items, quarterly adjustments, and viewing the Warlord as a "business problem." This advances her pragmatic supply-chain characterisation.
  - Rowan Delk (N-06) referenced in the Bandit Archer entry for reading wind (he and the archers use the same skill for different purposes).
  - Gavrik Stoneshoulder (N-08) referenced in the Lieutenant entry for guild contract pricing by rank, continuing the pattern from M-14 (warg contracts).
  - The Mysterious Stranger (N-12) delivers a lore hook in the Warlord entry: "He is building something." This connects to the Stranger's pattern of revealing larger narratives and suggests the Warlord's ambitions extend beyond banditry.
  - Pack Alpha (M-14) referenced in the Warlord's combat section: Battle Cry mirrors Howl mechanically, because leadership (human or animal) functions the same way. This creates a narrative parallel between the two tier 4 bosses that share the plains.
- **Bandit family site nouns** (Patrol, Camp, Stronghold) map directly: highway bandits run patrols, archers and lieutenants operate from camps, the Warlord commands from his stronghold.
- **Design theme:** The three Whispering Plains families occupy distinct tactical niches: wolves (physical pursuit), harpies (aerial evasion), bandits (human intelligence and fortification). The bandits are the only family that uses debuffs (Dirty Trick) and self-buffs (Battle Cry) in combination, reflecting their human capacity for dirty, strategic fighting.
- **Warlord as lore seed:** "He appeared three years ago" and "building something" are deliberate hooks. The Warlord's origin, his connection to Fen Darrow's former regiment, and his long-term plans are left open for future quest content or narrative expansion.
