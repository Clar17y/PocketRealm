# Mob Flavor Text: Serpents (Sunken Ruins)

## Content

### Family Overview

The serpents of the Sunken Ruins are the water's own predators: cold-blooded, patient, and perfectly adapted to the flooded corridors and submerged galleries that make the ruins lethal for anything that breathes air. The sea snakes and marsh vipers are animals, evolved for saltwater and hunting by heat and vibration. The naga are not. The naga are something else entirely: intelligent, tool-using, language-having beings who rule the deep water with a cold, territorial authority that predates Thornwall, predates the marsh, and may predate the ruins themselves.

Vesper Tain has a theory about the naga, one she has not published: that they are not the ruins' invaders but their inheritors. The carvings in the Throne Hall depict serpentine figures alongside the lost civilisation's people, not as enemies or servants but as equals. If Vesper is right, the naga were here before the water rose. They watched the civilisation build, and they watched it fall, and they kept what was left. The Naga Queen does not rule from the Throne Hall by conquest. She rules by succession.

---

### Sea Snake
*Appearance:* A sleek, muscular serpent approximately four feet long, with banded scales of dark grey and blue-green that render it nearly invisible in the ruins' murky water. Its head is narrow and blunt, its eyes adapted for the lightless corridors, and its mouth contains a single pair of curved fangs that fold flat against the jaw until the moment they do not. You see the sea snake when it strikes. By then, the relevant information has already arrived.
*Behavior:* Sea snakes coil between the submerged columns of the entrance halls, hunting by vibration and thermal sense. They are ambush predators: motionless in the water until prey passes within range, then fast enough to close the gap before you process the movement. Venom Strike on round 3 delivers 6 damage with a toxin that burns in the wound and lingers in a way that Vesper classifies as "pharmacologically interesting."
*Lore:* Sea snakes are the Sunken Ruins' most common predator and the first thing most adventurers encounter upon entering the flooded corridors. They are not venomous enough to be dangerous individually. They are, however, rarely individual. Rowan Delk, who has foraged Abyssal Kelp in the upper galleries, says the trick to avoiding sea snakes is to move slowly and never touch the columns. He also says nobody follows this advice, because "slowly" is not a speed that survives the first encounter with something else.
*Combat Feel:* Fast and evasive (accuracy 21, evasion 10) with low durability (HP 45, defence 14). Venom Strike on round 3 adds a damage spike. Sea snakes hit accurately and dodge well, but fold to sustained pressure. The tier 1 introduction to the ruins' combat philosophy: everything here is faster and more accurate than what came before.

### Marsh Viper
*Appearance:* Shorter and thicker than the sea snake, with a triangular head, slit-pupil eyes, and mottled brown-green scales that blend into the silt and debris of the flooded floors. It does not look aquatic. It looks like something that fell into the water and decided to make it a personality. The viper coils in the shallows, its body barely submerged, watching the surface with the patience of something that has nowhere to be and intends to stay.
*Behavior:* Marsh vipers hunt the shallower pools and flooded doorways of the mid-ruins, striking at anything that wades through their territory. No spells, no tricks; just the highest accuracy (22) and evasion (12) in the tier 1 serpent roster, delivered through pure, efficient physical violence. The viper does not chase. It does not retreat. It occupies a section of corridor and makes that section yours to deal with.
*Lore:* Marsh vipers are believed to have migrated into the Sunken Ruins from the Haunted Marsh, following the water table down through flooded tunnels. They have adapted to the saltwater with an efficiency that Vesper finds concerning, because species do not adapt this fast without environmental pressure, and whatever pressured the vipers into adapting is presumably still in the water with them.
*Combat Feel:* The purest physical glass cannon in the serpent family. Highest accuracy (22) and evasion (12) of the tier 1 trash, lowest HP (40) and defence (12). Every fight is a contest of whether you can connect before the viper chips you down with consistent, accurate strikes. No spells means no spikes; the danger is accumulation, not devastation.

### Naga Warrior
*Appearance:* From the waist up, the Naga Warrior could pass for human if you did not look at the eyes: cold, vertical-slit pupils in a face that is beautiful in the way that a blade is beautiful, which is to say not at all concerned with your opinion. From the waist down: a powerful serpentine body of iridescent scales that moves through the water with a muscular fluidity no human could match. The warrior carries a trident in one hand and a scale shield in the other, both crafted from materials that predate anything in Thornwall's armoury.
*Behavior:* Naga warriors guard the approaches to the inner sanctum, patrolling the deep galleries in pairs that communicate through sub-vocal clicks and gestures the water carries but human ears cannot interpret. Trident Thrust on round 3 delivers 11 physical damage with a weapon designed for underwater combat (the tines channel water pressure into the strike, which makes it hit harder submerged than on land). Scale Shield on round 5 buffs defence by 5 for three rounds, creating a durable window that forces you to either burst through it or wait it out.
*Lore:* The naga warriors are the Queen's military: disciplined, hierarchical, and operating with a chain of command that would make Captain Fen Darrow professionally envious. Their armour and weapons are not forged. They are grown, cultivated from the same mineral-rich water that forms the Naga Pearl (I-14). Vesper's examination of a recovered trident revealed that the metal is Ancient Ore, shaped without heat, which means the naga have a metalworking technique that does not involve a forge. Kessa Ironweld heard this and went very quiet for a long time.
*Combat Feel:* The elite. HP 70, defence 22, accuracy 24, magic defence 16, and a two-ability rotation that combines burst damage with defensive escalation. Trident Thrust (round 3) hits hard. Scale Shield (round 5) makes the warrior harder to kill just as the fight develops. The naga warrior is the Sunken Ruins' first intelligent combatant: it fights with technique, timing, and the awareness that you are not the first adventurer it has faced and will not be the last.

### Naga Queen (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Naga Queen is the largest naga in the Sunken Ruins, coiled on the dais of a flooded throne that was not built for her but which she occupies as if it were. Her scales are deep sapphire blue, edged with gold that catches the bioluminescent light and turns it into something that resembles a crown. She wears no armour. She does not need to; her scales are denser than anything Kessa has ever worked (the Naga Scale in I-14 is "dense as stone"), and they cover her from crown to tail-tip in overlapping plates that shift like water when she moves. Her eyes are gold, ancient, and carry the expression of something that has been waiting for you and is not impressed by what arrived.
*Behavior:* The Naga Queen fights with a four-spell rotation that is the most devastating damage sequence in the Pocketrealm. Tidal Blessing on round 2 buffs her attack by 6 for three rounds, the highest self-buff in the game. Water Jet on round 4 delivers 14 magic damage during the buff window. Constrict on round 6 drops your evasion by 5 for three rounds, eliminating your ability to dodge the finisher. Tsunami on round 8 delivers 24 magic damage, the single highest damage spell in the entire game: a wall of water called from the flooded galleries that hits with the force of the ocean remembering what it was.
*Lore:* The Naga Queen holds the deep water of the Sunken Ruins, and the deep water holds everything else. She and the Lich contest the Throne Hall (Z-11), but they do not fight. They coexist in the wary, territorial detente of two apex predators who have calculated the cost of conflict and found it unacceptable. The Mysterious Stranger, on one of their rarer visits, said: "The Lich commands the dead. The Queen commands the water. The ruins belong to whichever one you ask." Vesper's theory about the naga as inheritors begins and ends with the Queen: the Throne Hall's carvings depict a serpentine figure on the dais. The Queen sits where that figure sat. Draw your own conclusions.
*Combat Feel:* The serpent family boss and one of the Pocketrealm's three endgame bosses. HP 110, defence 28, accuracy 26, magic defence 22, and a four-spell rotation that escalates to the highest damage in the game. Tidal Blessing (round 2) creates the largest buff window. Water Jet (round 4) delivers magic damage during it. Constrict (round 6) pins you for the finisher. Tsunami (round 8) at 24 damage is the game's hardest single hit. The Queen demands everything: magic defence to survive the spells, physical damage to break through defence 28, enough health to absorb Tsunami if the fight goes long, and the strategic awareness that after round 8 the cycle begins again. She is not the hardest boss in the Sunken Ruins (that distinction belongs to the Eldritch Abomination). She is the most elegant. Every spell serves the next. Every round closes a door.

- Store as `bestiaryFlavorText` on the `MobTemplate` model, same pattern as all bestiary entries.
- **Family overview** establishes the serpent family as split between animal predators (snakes, vipers) and an intelligent civilisation (naga). This mirrors the Haunted Marsh's split between mindless beasts and intentional witches, but at a higher scale: the naga are not just intelligent, they are heirs to the ruins' lost civilisation.
- **Vesper's inheritance theory:** The biggest lore advancement in the bestiary. The naga are not invaders; they are inheritors. The Throne Hall carvings depict a serpentine figure on the dais, and the Queen sits where that figure sat. This connects to the Mysterious Stranger's lore about the civilisation that "wrote their history on the walls" (N-12) and reframes the naga from enemies to custodians. The theory is unconfirmed, leaving room for future narrative.
- **Naga cold-forging:** The naga shape Ancient Ore without heat, using a technique that does not involve a forge. This is a significant worldbuilding detail that makes Kessa "go very quiet," because it implies a metallurgy that exceeds her understanding. It connects to the Ancient Ore deposits (Z-11) and positions naga craftsmanship as T5-tier or beyond.
