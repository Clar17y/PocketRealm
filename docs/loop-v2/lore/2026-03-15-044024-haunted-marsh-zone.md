# Zone Atmosphere: Haunted Marsh

## Content

### Arrival Text

The ground softens under your boots, then squelches, then tries to keep them. The air thickens with the smell of decay: not the clean rot of a forest floor but the slow, wet dissolution of things that should have stayed buried. Mist clings to the surface of black water, and somewhere beneath it, something shifts. The Haunted Marsh does not welcome visitors. It tolerates them, briefly, the way a predator tolerates prey that has not yet noticed where it is standing.

### Ambient Descriptions

*Exploration (early):*
The marsh begins as soggy grassland, ankle-deep in brackish water that reflects a sky it should not be able to see through the mist. Dead trees stand at odd angles, their bark stripped white by moisture and time. Bog toads croak from unseen perches. The ground bubbles occasionally, releasing pockets of gas that smell like something you should not have been able to identify and wish you could not.

*Exploration (mid):*
Deeper. The water rises to your knees, and the ground beneath it becomes unreliable: solid stone one step, bottomless silt the next. Dark iron deposits jut from the muck in corroded veins, staining the water rust-red where they surface. Bogwood trunks lie half-submerged, their surfaces slick with something that is not moss and not mould and is best left unexamined. The mist has closed behind you. Thornwall's walls are no longer visible, and the sun is a rumour.

*Exploration (deep):*
The marsh becomes a labyrinth of stagnant channels and sunken islands. The dead walk here: skeletons rising from the mud with the unhurried patience of things that have nowhere else to be. The witches' territory begins at the thorn hedges that grow from the water itself, marking boundaries that the swamp's other inhabitants know better than to cross. The air tastes of iron and old magic, and the mist has begun to glow faintly, lit from below by something you do not want to find.

*After combat:*
The marsh fills the silence the way water fills a footprint: slowly, completely, without opinion. The mist settles back. The dead return to the mud, or do not, depending on how thoroughly you dealt with them. Nothing here stays dead reliably. That is the problem with the Haunted Marsh. It remembers.

### Environmental Flavor

**The Shallows:** Where the Whispering Plains give way to standing water and the grass turns from blue-green to grey-brown to dead. The transition is gradual enough that you are waist-deep before you realise you have entered the marsh proper. Thornwall's eastern patrols mark the boundary with iron stakes. The stakes rust within weeks. They replace them anyway.

**The Iron Mires:** Stretches of rust-coloured water where dark iron ore veins surface through the mud in thick, corroded seams. The ore is high-grade but extracting it means standing knee-deep in water that is cold, foul, and occasionally inhabited. Miners work in pairs here, one digging and one watching. The watching is considered the harder job.

**The Bogwood Groves:** Stands of dead and dying trees whose wood has been preserved by the marsh's chemistry into something darker, denser, and more resilient than any living timber. Bogwood does not rot. Gavrik Stoneshoulder says this is because even decay has standards. Harvesting it means wading into territory that the swamp beasts consider theirs, and their definition of "theirs" is expansive.

**The Gravemoss Banks:** Low, spongy mounds where Gravemoss grows in thick, grey-green carpets. The moss smells of damp earth and something medicinal, and Vesper Tain considers it essential for her Resist Potion formula. It grows best on the banks where the dead have been buried, which is either a coincidence or the marsh's idea of humour. Nobody laughs.

**The Coven Hollows:** Sunken clearings deep in the marsh where the witches have made their homes: crude huts of woven thorn and stretched hide, surrounded by bone chimes and the acrid smoke of whatever they are brewing. The hollows are difficult to find, which is by design, and more difficult to leave, which is also by design.

**The Bonefields:** A vast, shallow expanse where the marsh's bottom is carpeted with the remains of things that died here and were not allowed to stay dead. Bones surface through the mud in tangled heaps, and the skeletons that rise from them do so with a familiarity that suggests they have done this before. Gavrik lost his hand here. The memorial wall in Thornwall has three names from this stretch alone.

### Exploration Tier Flavor

*Tier 1 (0-25%):* The shallows. Skeletons rise from the mud with mechanical slowness. Zombies drag themselves from the water. Bog toads watch from the dark. Hag servants lurk at the edges, casting curses from behind thorn screens. The danger is real but manageable, if you watch your footing.

*Tier 2 (25-50%):* The mid-marsh. Marsh crawlers patrol the waterways. Cursed villagers attack with the desperate violence of people who were not always what they are now. The iron mires and bogwood groves are here, and so are the things that guard them.

*Tier 3 (50-75%):* Deep territory. Wraiths drift through the mist, draining life with a touch. Swamp hydras surface in the wider channels. Bog witches hold court in the Coven Hollows, hexing anything that approaches. The marsh has stopped tolerating you and started objecting.

*Tier 4 (75-100%):* The Bonefields and beyond. The Death Knight rides through the mist with armour that screams against itself. The Ancient Crocodile lies in the deepest channel, patient as geology. The Coven Mother weaves from the heart of the hollows, her magic older than the marsh itself. This is where Gavrik's patrol ended. This is where yours might, too.

## Integration Notes

- **Arrival text:** Display on first entry from Thornwall's eastern gate. The tonal shift from Thornwall's fortified discipline to the marsh's wet, rotting hostility should be jarring.
- **Ambient descriptions:** Rotate in exploration feed. The marsh's atmosphere is oppressive and biological, distinct from the Deep Forest's predatory watchfulness, the Cave Entrance's claustrophobic acoustics, and the Ancient Grove's sacred warmth.
- **Environmental flavor:** Sub-locations tied to game systems: Iron Mires (mining/Dark Iron Ore), Bogwood Groves (woodcutting/Bogwood Log), Gravemoss Banks (foraging/Gravemoss), Coven Hollows (witch encounters), The Bonefields (undead encounters), The Shallows (entry zone).
- **Cross-references:**
  - Gavrik Stoneshoulder (N-08) is referenced three times: bogwood joke, losing his hand in the Bonefields, and the memorial wall. This makes the Haunted Marsh personal for players who know Gavrik.
  - Vesper Tain (N-04) is referenced via Gravemoss and the Resist Potion (I-06).
  - Thornwall's eastern patrols (Z-08) are referenced in the Shallows.
  - T4 weapons (I-08): Dark Iron Greatsword and Bogwood Longbow materials are grounded in the Iron Mires and Bogwood Groves.
  - Tier flavors reference specific mobs: Skeleton, Zombie, Bog Toad, Hag Servant (tier 1), Marsh Crawler, Cursed Villager (tier 2), Wraith, Swamp Hydra, Bog Witch (tier 3), Death Knight, Ancient Crocodile, Coven Mother (tier 4).
- **Tone:** The Haunted Marsh is the Pocketrealm's first genuinely hostile zone. Previous zones were dangerous but survivable in spirit. The marsh is where things go to die and then refuse to stop. The humour is present but darker: the marsh's "idea of humour," decay having "standards," the Bonefields as a named location for a place where people are buried and then walk.
