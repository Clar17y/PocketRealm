# Mob Flavor Text: Spirits (Ancient Grove)

## Content

### Family Overview

The spirits of the Ancient Grove are not ghosts. Ghosts are dead things that forgot to leave. The spirits were never alive in the way that flesh understands the word. They are the grove's memory made visible: the residue of centuries of accumulated magic, shaped by the trees and the soil and the slow, patient turning of seasons into something that moves, thinks, and, when provoked, fights with a fury that has nothing to do with anger and everything to do with purpose. They do not hate you. They do not need to. You are trespassing in a place that predates the concept of trespassing, and the correction is impersonal.

---

### Forest Sprite
*Appearance:* A flickering, child-sized figure of pale golden light, vaguely humanoid, with features that shift and blur like a reflection in disturbed water. It leaves no footprints. It casts no shadow. It smells, faintly, of wildflowers and warm stone.
*Behavior:* Forest sprites drift through the outer grove in loose clusters, following the Spirit Paths with the unhurried pace of something that has nowhere specific to be and all of eternity to get there. They are not aggressive by nature, but they are curious, and their curiosity expresses itself as Sparkle (round 3): a burst of concentrated light that is beautiful, involuntary, and surprisingly painful. They do not seem to understand that it hurts. They also do not seem to care.
*Lore:* Vesper Tain theorises that forest sprites are fragments of the grove's ambient magic, condensed by the elderwood canopy into semi-autonomous forms. They are, in her words, "the forest dreaming with its eyes open." Whether they are conscious in any meaningful sense is a question that philosophers could debate for centuries. The sprites, for their part, have offered no comment.
*Combat Feel:* Magic damage, high evasion, low HP. They flicker in and out of targeting range, making physical attacks unreliable. Magic-resistant builds struggle too; their magicDefence of 12 is high for tier 1. The trick is committing to burst damage before they sparkle. If you hear the chime, you are already being hit.

### Wisp
*Appearance:* A small, intensely bright orb of blue-white light, no larger than a fist, that bobs and weaves through the air with the erratic grace of a candle flame in a draft. Looking directly at it for too long leaves afterimages that persist for minutes.
*Behavior:* Wisps are the grove's navigational hazards. They follow the Spirit Paths but frequently detour, drifting toward anything warm-blooded with a magnetism that feels deliberate. They do not attack with spells; they simply radiate magic damage through proximity, as if their existence is inherently incompatible with your comfort. Getting close enough to hit one means getting close enough to be burned by whatever they are made of.
*Lore:* The old legends say wisps are the souls of lost travellers, trapped forever in the grove's magic. Maren Ashwick tells this version at The Crooked Antler because it makes for a good story. The truth, as far as anyone can determine, is less romantic: wisps are condensed nodes of ambient energy that have developed a rudimentary attraction to body heat. The effect on the traveller, admittedly, is similar.
*Combat Feel:* The highest evasion in the spirit family (12) and the lowest HP (18). Pure magic damage, no spell pattern; just consistent, unavoidable radiative hurt. Wisps are less "fights" and more "weather" that you endure until it stops. Accuracy is everything. If you cannot hit them, you cannot kill them, and they will drain you one tick at a time.

### Dryad
*Appearance:* A tall, slender figure of living wood and woven vine, with features carved from pale bark and eyes of deep amber that hold a warmth the rest of her does not. Leaves grow from her shoulders and crown like a mantle, shifting colour with her mood: green for calm, gold for wary, red for the last thing you will see.
*Behavior:* Dryads are the grove's gardeners and its guardians, tending the elderwoods and the Starbloom with a patience that turns instantly to violence when their charges are threatened. Heal Self on round 3 mends wounds with a speed that suggests her body is closer to plant than person, regrowing damage the way a branch regrows bark. Thorn Burst on round 5 is less subtle: a detonation of razor-edged thorns that shred everything within reach. She heals first. Then she hurts. The order is deliberate.
*Lore:* Dryads are bound to specific trees in the grove, and the health of one reflects the health of the other. Cutting down a dryad's tree kills the dryad. Killing the dryad kills the tree. This is why Rowan Delk, who will cut down almost anything, refuses to fell elderwood in certain clearings. He will not explain which ones. He does not need to.
*Combat Feel:* The spirit family's mid-boss and the first encounter that punishes slow fights. Heal Self on round 3 undoes your progress if you have not frontloaded damage. Thorn Burst on round 5 adds a damage spike. Magic defence of 18 means magical approaches are contested; she is resistant to her own element. The best strategy is overwhelming physical burst before round 3, or accepting a long, attrition-heavy fight that she is better equipped for than you.

### Ancient Spirit (Boss)
*Appearance:* A towering column of golden light in a vaguely human shape, twice the height of a person and rippling with power that makes the air itself hum. Its features are indistinct but its presence is absolute: the temperature drops, the elderwoods lean inward, and the Starbloom at its feet blazes like fallen stars. It does not walk. It arrives.
*Behavior:* The Ancient Spirit is the oldest and most powerful entity in the grove, dwelling at the heart of the Sanctum. It opens with Spirit Shield on round 2 (a barrier of condensed light that raises its already formidable defence by 4 for three rounds), transitions to Soul Drain on round 4 (a targeted extraction of vitality that deals significant damage), and culminates in Wrath on round 7 (a concentrated release of accumulated grove magic that hits with the force of something that has been holding back until now). Between abilities, its standard attacks deal magic damage that ignores most physical mitigation. It fights the way an earthquake fights: without malice, without hesitation, and without any particular interest in whether you survive.
*Lore:* The Ancient Spirit is the grove's will given form. Not its ruler (the grove has no ruler; it is its own authority) but its voice, its memory, and its final answer to anyone who has pushed past the sprites, the wisps, the dryads, and the treants and still refuses to leave. Maren Ashwick's claim that the Alpha Wolf is older than the Deep Forest may or may not be true. Nobody makes similar claims about the Ancient Spirit, because nobody disputes them. The Spirit Essence trophy dropped by its defeat is one of the rarest materials in the Pocketrealm, and the Spirit Staff forged from it carries an echo of the grove's magic that Vesper Tain has described, with uncharacteristic brevity, as "alive."
*Combat Feel:* A true boss encounter with 1400 base HP and the highest magic defence in the game at its tier. Spirit Shield (round 2) turns an already durable enemy into a fortress. Soul Drain (round 4) deals damage that you cannot afford if the fight is already costing resources. Wrath (round 7) is the check: if you have not broken through the shield phase and dealt meaningful damage by round 7, you will not survive what comes next. Physical damage is the recommended approach (its physical defence is lower than its magical defence), but even that requires serious gear. This is not a fight you stumble into. This is a fight you prepare for, and the preparation is its own kind of respect.

## Integration Notes

- Store as `bestiaryFlavorText` on the `MobTemplate` model, same pattern as all bestiary entries.
- **Family overview** establishes spirits as fundamentally different from other mob families: not alive, not dead, not hostile in a personal sense. The grove's correction is "impersonal," which makes it more unsettling than rage.
- **Forest Sprite** references Vesper Tain (N-04, "the forest dreaming with its eyes open") and the Spirit Paths (Z-05).
- **Wisp** references Maren Ashwick (N-03) telling the romantic version at The Crooked Antler.
- **Dryad** references Rowan Delk (N-06) refusing to fell certain elderwoods, adding depth to his land-respect philosophy and hinting at personal encounters with dryads.
- **Ancient Spirit** references the Sanctum (Z-05), the boss achievement lore (A-02), Spirit Essence trophy, Spirit Staff (boss gear from items.ts), Maren's Alpha Wolf claim (M-04), and Vesper's brevity (mirroring the Lich Staff description from I-09, creating a pattern: Vesper goes quiet when things are genuinely beyond her).
- Spirit family site nouns (Cluster, Circle, Sanctum) map naturally: sprites in clusters, dryads in circles, the Ancient Spirit in the Sanctum.
- **Design theme:** All spirits deal magic damage and have high evasion and magicDefence. The lore explains this as their nature: they are made of magic, resistant to magic, and difficult to hit because they are not entirely physical. Physical attacks are the counter, which the Ancient Spirit entry states directly.
