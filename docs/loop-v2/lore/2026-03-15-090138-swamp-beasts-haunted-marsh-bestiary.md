# Mob Flavor Text: Swamp Beasts (Haunted Marsh)

## Content

### Family Overview

The swamp beasts of the Haunted Marsh are not undead, not cursed, and not magical. They are simply alive, in a place that kills most things, and they have been alive here for longer than anyone has been keeping records. The marsh's ambient necromancy does not affect them. Vesper Tain theorises that the same chemistry which preserves the dead has, over generations, rendered the living immune: the toads, crawlers, hydras, and crocodiles have adapted to the marsh's magic by ignoring it entirely. This makes them the most honest threat in the zone. They are not animated by dark forces. They are just very large, very territorial, and very hungry.

Gavrik Stoneshoulder's guild contracts list swamp beasts under "natural hazards," which is technically accurate and practically unhelpful. The Bogwood Groves, the Iron Mires, and the deeper channels are their territory, and they defend it with the patient aggression of things that were here before the undead and will be here after.

---

### Bog Toad
*Appearance:* Roughly the size of a large dog, with mottled grey-green skin, bulging amber eyes, and a mouth that opens wider than anything that size has a right to. It sits motionless on mud banks and fallen bogwood logs, indistinguishable from a rock until the rock blinks. Its throat sac inflates when it is about to attack, which is the only warning you get and, given the speed of what follows, not much of one.
*Behavior:* Bog toads are ambush predators. They sit, they wait, they strike with a tongue that moves faster than you can react and hits harder than a tongue should be able to. Tongue Lash on round 3 deals 5 magic damage from a muscular appendage that Vesper classifies as "technically a limb and practically a weapon." Between lashes, the bog toad is a moderate physical threat: tough enough to absorb punishment, accurate enough to connect, and ugly enough to make you want to finish the fight quickly for aesthetic reasons.
*Lore:* Bog toads have been in the marsh for as long as the marsh has existed, which by most estimates is longer than Thornwall. Their Bog Hearts (I-13) pulse for hours after extraction, a property that Vesper attributes to chemical reactions and adventurers attribute to the marsh being fundamentally wrong about how biology works. Rowan Delk says the easiest way to avoid a bog toad is to watch for tongue impressions in the mud. He also says this requires looking at the mud, which in the Haunted Marsh is its own punishment.
*Combat Feel:* Tier 1 entry with a twist. HP 45, defence 14, accuracy 16, and Tongue Lash (round 3, 5 magic damage) make the bog toad sturdier and more dangerous than the undead trash tier. The magic damage on Tongue Lash bypasses physical armour, creating an early lesson that the marsh's beasts demand different preparation than its skeletons.

### Marsh Crawler
*Appearance:* A broad, low-slung reptile approximately six feet long, armoured in overlapping plates of mud-darkened chitin, with short, powerful legs built for traction on soft ground. Its head is flat and blunt, its jaw is lined with serrated ridges rather than teeth, and its eyes sit on top of its skull, level with the waterline. You see the eyes. You do not see the rest. That is the design working as intended.
*Behavior:* Marsh crawlers patrol the waterways between the Bogwood Groves and the Iron Mires, moving through shin-deep water with a slow, deliberate gait that is exactly as fast as it needs to be (which is faster than you, in the mud). They are territorial rather than predatory: they will not chase you, but they will not let you pass, and the difference between these positions narrows quickly when you are knee-deep in their channel. No spells. No tricks. Just defence (18) and the patience of something that does not need to be clever to win.
*Lore:* Miners working the Iron Mires consider marsh crawlers a routine hazard, in the same way that one might consider the tide routine. Gavrik's guild contracts for the Mires include a standard warning: "Crawlers claim the water. Work from the banks." The Bog Heart harvested from them (I-13) is identical to the bog toad's, which Vesper finds puzzling and Kessa finds irrelevant, because she does not use organs and the crawler's chitin is not metal enough for her standards.
*Combat Feel:* The defensive wall. Highest defence (18) of the tier 2 marsh mobs, with HP 50 and almost no evasion (2). Marsh crawlers absorb damage and hit back steadily. There are no spikes, no debuffs, and no surprises. The fight is a straightforward test of whether your sustained damage output exceeds their sustained durability. It usually takes longer than you want.

### Swamp Hydra
*Appearance:* Three heads on thick, muscular necks rising from a body that is mostly submerged and best imagined from the damage it causes rather than observed directly. The heads are serpentine, scaled in dark green and black, each one capable of independent movement and each one watching a different direction. The body, when it surfaces, is the size of a small boat and approximately as welcome in the channel you were trying to wade through. Its scales shed constantly, and the discarded Hydra Scales (I-13) are so dense that they sink rather than float, which says something about the creature wearing them.
*Behavior:* Swamp hydras occupy the wider channels of the mid-marsh, surfacing without warning in stretches of water that were empty a moment ago. Acid Spray on round 3 deals 8 damage as one head vomits a stream of corrosive bile that smokes on contact with armour and flesh alike. Regenerate on round 5 heals 6 HP, because the hydra's biology has decided that fairness is optional and recovery is mandatory. Between spells, it bites with whichever head is closest, and three heads means something is always closest.
*Lore:* Kessa Ironweld works Hydra Scale into shields and heavy armour, and she says the only material harder to work is "the hydra itself" (I-13), which is the closest she comes to admitting respect for a living thing. The hydra's regeneration is biological rather than magical, which means it cannot be dispelled. The hydra simply grows back what it loses, given time. The only reliable counter is not giving it time.
*Combat Feel:* The elite threat. HP 80, defence 20, accuracy 18, and a two-spell rotation that combines offence and sustain. Acid Spray (round 3) deals a flat 8 damage that punishes anyone relying on physical armour alone. Regenerate (round 5) heals 6, extending the fight and punishing slow damage output. The hydra rewards burst strategies: hit hard, hit fast, kill it before the healing cycle gives back what you took. Low evasion (3) means you will connect. Whether you connect hard enough is the question.

### Ancient Crocodile (Boss)
*Appearance:* The Ancient Crocodile is not a crocodile that grew old. It is a geological feature that learned to bite. Thirty feet of armoured predator lying in the deepest channel of the Haunted Marsh, its hide so encrusted with mud, algae, and mineral deposits that the waterline cannot tell where the bank ends and the crocodile begins. Its eyes are golden, ancient, and completely still. They have been watching this channel for longer than Thornwall has existed. They are watching you now with the disinterested assessment of something that has eaten everything the marsh has ever produced and found none of it difficult.
*Behavior:* The Ancient Crocodile fights with the unhurried violence of something that has never needed to rush. Death Roll on round 3 delivers 12 physical damage as it seizes and spins with a force that armour was not designed to withstand (armour was designed by people; the crocodile predates the concept). Submerge on round 5 drops it below the waterline, boosting evasion by 5 for two rounds as you swing at turbid water and miss. Jaws on round 7 is 18 damage delivered with the full closing force of a mouth that was ancient when the marsh was young. The crocodile does not chase. It does not need to. You are in its water. You are already within range.
*Lore:* The Ancient Crocodile appears in Thornwall's maps with the label "confirmed resident," the same designation as the Pack Alpha and the Death Knight. Gavrik's contracts for the deep channels pay triple the base rate, and even then, takers are few. Croc Hide (I-13) from this creature is a material Kessa describes as "less leather, more geological deposit," and the armour made from it carries the faint, permanent smell of the marsh. Captain Fen Darrow's patrol maps mark the crocodile's channel with a single word: "No." The word has not needed updating.
*Combat Feel:* The swamp beasts boss. HP 110, defence 26, accuracy 20, and a three-spell rotation that combines devastating physical damage with a defensive phase. Death Roll (round 3) is the opening statement: 12 damage that announces the crocodile is not negotiating. Submerge (round 5) creates a two-round evasion window that wastes your attacks at the worst possible time. Jaws (round 7) closes the argument with 18 damage. The Ancient Crocodile has the highest defence in the Haunted Marsh (26) and the lowest evasion (1, when not submerged), which means every hit lands and every hit matters less than you hoped. This is a siege. Bring potions. Bring patience. Bring the understanding that you are not the apex predator here.

## Integration Notes

- Store as `bestiaryFlavorText` on the `MobTemplate` model, same pattern as all bestiary entries.
- **Family overview** establishes that swamp beasts are natural animals immune to the marsh's necromancy, not supernatural creatures. This makes them the Haunted Marsh's biological baseline: the things that were here before the dead started walking and will be here after.
- **Design theme: physical durability.** Every member of the family has high defence, high HP, and minimal evasion. They are the "wall" family, contrasting with the undead's attrition-and-magic theme (M-16) and the upcoming witches' debuff-and-curse theme (M-18). The swamp beasts force pure sustained damage checks.
- **Cross-references:**
  - Vesper Tain (N-04) anchors the scientific perspective: immunity theory (overview), Tongue Lash classification (bog toad), Bog Heart chemistry (I-13 callback), and hydra regeneration being biological rather than magical. Her expertise is the lens through which the beasts' biology is understood.
  - Rowan Delk (N-06) referenced in the bog toad entry for mud-reading, continuing his role as the practical survivalist who knows the terrain better than anyone and enjoys it less.
  - Gavrik Stoneshoulder (N-08) referenced in the overview (guild contracts), marsh crawler (Mires warning), and Ancient Crocodile (triple-rate contracts). The contracts create a through-line of institutional knowledge: the guild has learned how to price each beast.
  - Kessa Ironweld (N-02) referenced in the marsh crawler (chitin not metal enough), hydra (hardest material to work), and Ancient Crocodile (geological deposit). Her materials expertise provides scale.
  - Captain Fen Darrow (N-11) referenced in the Ancient Crocodile with his patrol map notation: "No." One word, needing no update.
  - Bog Heart (I-13), Hydra Scale (I-13), and Croc Hide (I-13) are all referenced, connecting each beast to its material output and the descriptions already established.
  - Bogwood Groves and Iron Mires (Z-09) are referenced as territorial boundaries.
- **Swamp beasts family site nouns** (Pool, Mire, Depths) map naturally: bog toads in pools, marsh crawlers in mires, the Ancient Crocodile in the depths.
- **Ancient Crocodile as apex predator:** The description deliberately frames the crocodile as the oldest, most permanent resident of the marsh. It predates Thornwall, the undead, and arguably the marsh's necromancy. Where the Death Knight (M-16) serves something unknown, the Ancient Crocodile serves nothing. It simply is. This makes it the Haunted Marsh's most alien boss: not evil, not magical, just ancient and perfectly adapted and utterly indifferent to your plans.
