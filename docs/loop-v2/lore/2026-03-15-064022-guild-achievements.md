# Achievement Flavor: Guild Milestones

## Content

### Guild Level Track

#### Guild Founder (guild level 5)

*"Five levels. Your guild has survived its infancy, which in the Pocketrealm means surviving the part where everyone argues about the name, the tax rate, and whose idea it was to recruit that one person who has not logged in since joining. The organisation stage is over. The building stage begins."*

Unlock text: Guild level 5. You are no longer a group of adventurers with a shared name. You are a guild, and the Pocketrealm has noticed.

#### Guild Builder (guild level 15, title: Guild Builder)

*"Fifteen levels. The guild has a treasury that means something, a roster that holds, and a reputation that precedes it. Contracts come in and get completed. Members contribute without being asked. Gavrik Stoneshoulder has started nodding when your guild's name comes up, which from him is the equivalent of a standing ovation."*

Unlock text: Level 15. Your guild is no longer growing. It is established. The difference is that established things are harder to remove.

#### Guildmaster (guild level 30, title: Guildmaster, +2 attribute points)

*"Thirty. Your guild has outlasted quarrels, roster turnover, failed contracts, and at least one treasury crisis that nobody talks about anymore. It has become an institution, the kind of thing that Thornwall's guard references in briefings and Millbrook's merchants factor into their quarterly projections. Gavrik updates the guild's file with his metal hand and a rare smile. The memorial wall behind him has fewer names because of guilds like yours. That is the real achievement."*

Unlock text: Guildmaster. Not a title you claim. A title your guild earned, and you earned by holding it together.

### Contract Track (selected)

#### Contract Worker (1 guild contract completed)

*"Your first contract, completed. The bounty was collective, the effort was shared, and the reward went to the treasury rather than your pocket. That is the strange alchemy of guild work: you do more, earn less personally, and somehow feel richer for it. Gavrik calls this 'the point.' He is, annoyingly, correct."*

Unlock text: One contract down. The guild works because people like you show up and do the work that nobody writes songs about.

### Contribution Track (selected)

#### Pillar of the Guild (100,000 turns contributed, title: Guild Pillar)

*"One hundred thousand turns, donated to the treasury. Not spent on your gear, your potions, or your next forge upgrade. Given, freely, to something larger than yourself. The guild's projects run on contributions like yours: the specialisations, the boosts, the upgrades that benefit everyone. You are not the guild's strongest fighter or its best crafter. You are something more important. You are its foundation."*

Unlock text: A hundred thousand turns, given willingly. The Pillar. Every guild needs someone who builds the floor that everyone else stands on. That someone is you.

## Integration Notes

- **Same display pattern as all previous achievement entries:** Short unlock text as toast/popup; italic passage in achievement detail view.
- **Three tracks represented:** Guild level (the guild's growth), contracts (collective work), contribution (personal sacrifice). Each has a different emotional register: level achievements are about organisational resilience, contracts are about showing up, contributions are about generosity.
- **Cross-references:**
  - Gavrik Stoneshoulder (N-08) is referenced in Guild Builder (his nod), Guildmaster (his metal hand and rare smile, the memorial wall), and Contract Worker ("the point"). He is the NPC most connected to guild identity, and these achievements reflect his values.
  - The memorial wall (first established in N-08) returns in Guildmaster, connecting guild success to the cost that motivates it.
  - Thornwall's guard and Millbrook's merchants are referenced in Guildmaster to show how a level 30 guild impacts the broader economy and security.
- **Cooperative tone:** Guild achievements are the only category that explicitly rewards collective effort. The writing emphasises "we" over "I" without being sentimental. The humour comes from the messiness of collaboration (arguing about names, inactive members, treasury crises) rather than from individual prowess.
- **Pillar distinction:** The Pillar achievement deliberately distinguishes itself from combat and crafting contributions. The player is not the strongest or the best; they are the most generous. This is positioned as equally (or more) valuable, which reinforces the guild system's cooperative design.
